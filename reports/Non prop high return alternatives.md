# Every non-prop subsidy turns into a race

No mechanism found in this research has evidence that a Malaysian resident can repeatably turn USD 10,000–15,000 into USD 15,000–20,000 a year outside prop firms without relying on creativity, gambling or rule evasion. The plain answer is "none found", and the gap is structural, not a matter of searching harder. The two closest candidates fail on different tests. **Lending-liquidation bonuses on smaller chains** are the most prop-like mechanism: the protocol pays a fixed bonus by rule, the bot can run again and again, and the downside per attempt is capped. The repo measured **USD 1.09–1.70M a month** going to winners outside the top three on seven venues. But a perfectly fast newcomer could reach only **10–13% of that value**, and the current estimate for a new bot is **USD 0–100 a month**. **Hedged points farming** is the only category with documented >100%-annualised episodes for single-wallet small players (Ethena Season 1 paid an estimated **+26% to ~+103% in six weeks**). Yet **85–93% of 2024–26 token launches trade below their launch price**, and no dataset counts the programmes that paid nothing, so it fails the repeatability test. **CoW Protocol solving** (about **USD 3.7M a year** in rewards) is the best-documented protocol-paid role that nobody has measured for a newcomer yet. Everything else tops out between roughly zero and 40% a year for a small player: maker rebates, MEV, intent filling, DePIN, Malaysian bank and broker promotions, and IPO ballots. The prop firm differs in one way. Its subsidy is **non-rival and renewable**: other customers or bots cannot shrink it, and a failed account can be bought again. Every non-prop subsidy found is either a fixed pool contested by faster specialists or a one-off per-person cap. The practical conclusion has three parts. Keep the capital in the Malaysia-eligible prop portfolio (about **USD 18,006 a year on about USD 13,858** of cash at risk in backtest). Let the liquidation shadow test finish. Run four cheap pre-registered measurements before closing the search. One caveat: the research sandbox could not open many primary pages, so most external figures below come from search-result extracts and need re-checking before they enter `docs/FIGURES_LEDGER.md`.

## Prop recycling pays because nobody races for the subsidy

Prop-firm recycling works because failing customers fund the payouts. The repo's coin-flip test shows that the Topstep 50K rules pay even random trades: 98% of coin-flip runs were profitable, with a median of USD 18,046 over 2016–26 against USD 44,359 real net for the friend's strategy. The four-firm Malaysia-eligible portfolio produced **USD 18,006 a year with about USD 13,858 of cash needed for the worst dip** ([START_HERE](../docs/START_HERE.md)). Three properties carry that result, and they are the yardstick for everything below. The subsidy is *non-rival*: a faster bot elsewhere does not shrink the payout on your account. It is *renewable*: a failed account is bought again and the same setup runs again. And the loss per attempt is *capped* at the evaluation fee.

Every non-prop pie large enough to matter fails the first property, and the measured concentration is extreme. Three searchers took about three-quarters of Ethereum CEX-DEX arbitrage value from August 2023 to March 2025 ([arXiv 2507.13023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13023v2)). The top three CoW solvers handled more than 50% of volume in early 2025 ([Sprinter](http://blog.sprinter.tech/building-economic-trust-in-solver-based-networks-part-2/)). Two firms, SCP and Wintermute, filled more than 90% of UniswapX volume ([arXiv 2503.00738](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00738v2)). Selini and Wintermute won more than 90% of Arbitrum Timeboost auction rounds ([arXiv 2509.22143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22143)). Two entities produce more than 80% of spam MEV on Base ([Flashbots](https://writings.flashbots.net/mev-and-the-limits-of-scaling)). Even the older dYdX liquidity-provider reward scheme sent **80.5% of rewards to the top two accounts** under one scoring variant ([dYdX Foundation](https://www.dydx.foundation/blog/liquidity-provider-rewards)). Where a pie is contested, the margin goes to whoever controls inclusion in the block: exclusive Ethereum searchers share most of their extracted value with affiliated block builders and sometimes run at negative net profit ([arXiv 2507.13023v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13023v3)). A new small bot therefore dilutes an existing pie rather than claiming a new one.

The non-rival subsidies that do exist come with per-person caps, so they do not scale with capital. Malaysian broker welcome packs are once per person: Moomoo's was "up to RM 1,800+" ([Moomoo MY](https://www.moomoo.com/my/support/topic9_545)), and Webull paid RM 200 of NVIDIA shares for RM 1,000 held 60 days ([FMT](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2026/03/26/webull-malaysia-offering-up-to-6-p-a-returns-and-free-nvidia-shares)). RHB's 0% balance transfer is for new cardholders only and capped at RM 30,000 ([RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/cards/credit-cards/balance-transfer)). i-Saraan matching tops out at RM 500 a year ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/10/11/budget-2026-simplified-what-malaysians-can-expect-in-tax-reliefs-cash-aids-and-other-benefits/194177)). Bursa IPO ballots allow one application per person per IPO ([repo #49](../docs/PREREG_LOOPHOLES_20261120.md)). This fits the repo's own record. About 100 non-prop ideas have been tested and none passed a full test ([START_HERE](../docs/START_HERE.md)). The best market-pricing result, cross-venue funding gaps (#208), passed at only **20.3% / 11.9% a year** with maker fills and was fragile. The Hyperliquid HLP vault (#205) was a near-miss at 27.7% / 7.9% ([CANDIDATES](../docs/CANDIDATES.md)). The repo's handoff note reached the same lesson: every Topstep-like return found so far was a subsidy built into someone's rules, not a price pattern ([HANDOFF_NONPROP](../docs/HANDOFF_NONPROP.md)).

## Ten candidates ranked; none shows repeatable 100% returns

Each candidate is scored 1–3 on three factors, and the scores are multiplied (maximum 27). The scores are my judgement from the evidence cited. **E (evidence)** asks whether a small, new player can reach about USD 15–20K a year. It is scored against dollars, not percentage return, because flash-loan strategies need almost no capital and would pass any percentage test trivially. 3 = documented small-player cases at or above the target, even if one-off; 2 = the pie and the arithmetic allow it and incumbents reach it, but there is no newcomer evidence; 1 = the evidence points well below the target. **T (testability)** asks whether free public data exists: 3 = free on-chain or official data, ideally already in the repo's pipeline; 2 = partial or hand-collected; 1 = none. **L (legality and access from Malaysia)** asks whether a resident can take part without breaking a rule: 3 = Malaysian-regulated, or permissionless contract calls with no restriction found; 2 = probably accessible, but the venue's terms or the KYC route are unverified; 1 = the host is on the Securities Commission (SC) Investor Alert List or under an SC order, or the activity is gambling-adjacent. Evidence labels in the tables: **[repo]** = measured in this repo with a reproducible command; **[paper]** = academic paper (abstract-level); **[official]** = the venue's own page, often read via a search extract; **[3rd]** = media, aggregator or blog; **[inf]** = my arithmetic from cited inputs.

Several mechanisms are excluded outright rather than ranked. Trading competitions are mostly hosted by exchanges the SC has listed or acted against: MEXC has been on the Investor Alert List since 2022 ([SC](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/investor-alert-updates/investor-alert-list-as-at-592022)), Bybit was ordered to shut its Malaysian access in December 2024 ([SC](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-takes-enforcement-action-against-bybit-for-illegally-operating-dax-in-malaysia)), and Binance was re-added to the list on 8 October 2026 ([SC](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/investor-alert-updates/investor-alert-list-as-at-8102026)). Their ROI-ranked prize tables are lotteries in any case: Bitget's USD 6M KCGI 2025 pool across 120,197 participants averages about USD 50 a head ([buycrypt](https://buycrypt.com/trading-competitions/)). Polymarket-linked UMA proposing is gambling-adjacent and whitelisted since August 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/366507/polymarket-uma-oracle-update)). It pays USD 2–5 a proposal against a USD 750 bond, which means a break-even accuracy of about 99.3% ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution)). Pump.fun sniping is excluded because its 87% win rate belongs to wallets funded by the token's own deployer, which is insider coordination, not an edge ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)). Pyth publishing is permissioned and its staking rewards are paused ([Pyth](https://docs.pyth.network/oracle-integrity-staking)). 1inch Fusion admits only ten whitelisted resolvers ([1inch](https://help.1inch.com/en/articles/6800254-1inch-fusion-faq)). Cross-exchange price gaps between centralised exchanges "hardly exist since April 2018" ([Crépellière et al.](https://wiwi.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin-wiwi/cetar/TAF_Working_Paper_Series/TAF_WP_067_CrepellierePelsterZeisberger2022_rev.pdf)).

## Liquidations and early points pools come closest, for opposite reasons

Liquidation bonuses pass every structural test and fail on capture. The repo's pre-registered 24-month test across 67 venues (#100) found seven that pass. On them, winners outside the top three collected **USD 1.70M and USD 1.09M a month** in the two halves, and the median active small bot earned USD 30–800 a month per venue before flash-loan fees and slippage [repo] ([CANDIDATES](../docs/CANDIDATES.md)). The replay tests then showed where that money goes. **64–78% of Aave-fork liquidations land in the same block as the price update that triggers them**, leaving a perfectly fast block-level watcher only **13% of events and 10% of value** ([PREREG_LIQ_REPLAY](../docs/PREREG_LIQ_REPLAY_20261121.md)). On Morpho Base, 93.8% land in the oracle-update block and the reachable share falls to 2.1% of events and 0.8% of value ([PREREG_LIQ_MORPHO_REPLAY](../docs/PREREG_LIQ_MORPHO_REPLAY_20261121.md)). The best-case check put parity with incumbents at USD 1.5–3.2K a month, which would meet the target, but found a median bot income of USD 0 ([PREREG_LIQ_BESTCASE](../docs/PREREG_LIQ_BESTCASE_20261122.md)). The repo's current estimate is USD 0–100 a month now and USD 100–600 averaged over crash cycles ([START_HERE](../docs/START_HERE.md)). Income is lumpy (the top five months produced 70% of the bonus), so a calm-market sample understates the long-run average. The smaller pies the specialists ignore are tiny: Mantle paid USD 162K in 24 months, or USD 64–147 per active bot-month, and Compound v3 has been effectively dead since March 2026 at USD 13 per bot-month ([CANDIDATES](../docs/CANDIDATES.md)). That is the general squeeze in one example. A pie too small for professionals to bother with is almost always too small to pay USD 15K a year.

Hedged points farming has the reverse profile: real >100% episodes, but no repeatability. A token issuer that pays early users from a fixed weekly points pool is a genuine third-party subsidy, like a prop firm's failing customers. Ethena's first season shows the scale. **750M ENA were spread across about 360.7B shards** ([AirdropAlert](https://airdropalert.com/airdrops/ethena/); [DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/defi/ethena-500m-airdrop-leads-defi-project-to-plan-the-next-one/)), with shard rates of 5 per dollar per day for holding USDe and up to 20 for locked Curve LP ([Bitpinas](https://bitpinas.com/learn-how-to-guides/ethena-airdrop-protocol/)). At a conservative USD 0.59 launch price, a USD 10K day-one holder earned about **USD 2,576 (+26%) in six weeks**, and a boosted LP position up to about **USD 10,305 (~+103%)**. These figures are the research notes' arithmetic on third-party inputs, consistent with an independent 23.75% estimate for plain holding ([Medium](https://medium.com/@stephenoresco/shards-out-sats-in-farming-ethenas-season-2-sats-campaign-e094bfa690f9)). Hyperliquid's 2024 genesis drop gave 310M HYPE to about 94,000 wallets at a USD 3.90 launch price, a mean of roughly **USD 12.9K per wallet** that is heavily skewed by large wallets ([Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15039718-hyperliquid-airdrop-hype-genesis-facts-and-season-2-status)). Lighter's points were worth about USD 49–60 each around launch, a range that spans LIT's 30% first-day fall ([Lighterpedia](https://lighterpedia.com/ecosystem/lighter-points-program); [MEXC](https://blog.mexc.com/news/lighter-lit-token-crashes-30-launch-price-2-45-airdrop-dump/)).

The systematic data cut the other way. **Only 8 of 62 airdrops in 2024 had positive returns after 90 days** ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/airdrops-in-the-barren-desert/)). **84.7% of 118 TGEs in 2025 traded below their launch valuation**, with a median fall in fully diluted value of about 71% ([Memento Research](https://mementoresearch.com/state-of-2025-token-launches-year-in-review)). CryptoRank puts the figure at 93% for 2024–26 tokens that reached a USD 100M market cap ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/airdrop-analysis-token-decline-strategy/)). The per-wallet medians people quote count only survivors. Dragonfly's **USD 4,600 median claim per eligible wallet** covers 12 airdrops that launched valuable tokens ([Dragonfly](https://www.sec.gov/files/dragonflys-state-airdrops-report-2025.pdf)). It leaves out programmes that paid nothing, wallets filtered as sybils (one person running many wallets), and capital or costs. That is why the repo's own pre-registration (#360) found the question "not measurable as registered" at Stage 0 ([PREREG_AIRDROP](../docs/PREREG_AIRDROP_20261123.md)). The 2026 pipeline is crowded and already late. Extended took its snapshot on 6 November 2026 ([AirdropAlert](https://airdropalert.com/blogs/xten-tokenomics/)). Variational offers 32% of supply but only 150,000 points a week ([The Blockverse](https://theblockverse.co/variational-airdrop/)). Guides already describe some programmes as "late", and several perp DEXs slash points retroactively for wash trading ([Dropstab](https://news.dropstab.com/research/top-perpetual-dex-platforms-for-point-farming)). Professional market makers farm the same pools at lower cost. Jump Crypto received about 9.6M LIT, worth more than USD 24M, at Lighter ([MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/news/386269)). Third-party summaries say Hyperliquid does not list Malaysia as restricted ([CoinPerps](https://www.coinperps.com/learn/hyperliquid-restricted-countries)), but KuCoin's HODLer airdrops explicitly exclude it ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/support/48142946141378)), so each programme's terms must be read. The honest reading is that early weeks of fixed pools have paid more than 100% annualised, and that no one has shown the median programme, counted with its zeros, pays at all.

## Protocol-paid roles pay a professional few

CoW Protocol is the one protocol-paid role where real money, a long tail of operators and free per-operator data coincide. From 1 January to 14 September 2026 the rewards safe paid solvers about **36.8M COW (≈ USD 2.6M)** on USD 23.3B of settled volume, about **USD 3.7M a year** ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/cow-swap-solver-auctions)). A search summary of the same piece attributed 52% to one team, but a second search could not confirm it. Twenty-eight solvers were active in early 2025 and 12 had more than 1% of volume ([Sprinter](http://blog.sprinter.tech/building-economic-trust-in-solver-based-networks-part-2/)). After CIP-74 tied rewards to protocol fees, concentration by trade count fell while concentration by volume rose from an HHI of 0.176 to 0.241 ([arXiv 2607.21955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21955)). Small orders became more contestable but carry small, fee-capped rewards, and some solvers say those rewards fall below overhead ([Blockchain Dose](https://blockchaindose.com/cow-protocol-and-anoma/)). The capital barrier is avoidable. The reduced bond is USD 50K plus 500K COW, about USD 85K at 2026 prices ([CIP-44](https://forum.cow.fi/t/cip-44-reduced-bonding-requirements/2424)), but the CoW DAO pool route needs only KYC and core-team approval ([CoW docs](https://cowswap.mintlify.app/cow-protocol/howto/solvers/onboard)). The binding constraint is solver quality, which is a routing-optimisation engineering race. If the top team does take about half, the remaining ~USD 1.85M split among ~25 solvers averages ~USD 74K each [inf]. That average would be skewed, gross of infrastructure costs, and unverified. It is the only number in this report that leaves room for a newcomer above the target, which is why CoW ranks third despite no small-solver income evidence.

Order-flow filling and arbitrage show the same concentration without CoW's long tail. Across relayers front their own capital and are repaid after a settlement delay of about 1.5 hours ([Across docs](https://docs.across.to/user-docs/how-across-works/speed)). An exclusivity layer then picks relayers partly by their destination-chain balances ([Across docs](https://docs.across.to/relayers/relayer-exclusivity)), which favours large inventories. Across says 32 relayers each cleared more than USD 10K of volume a week in Q2 2025 ([Across](https://x.com/AcrossProtocol/status/1949926734389784953)). That is volume, not profit. At an assumed 1–3 bp net margin, USD 15K a year requires USD 50–150M of fills, about 11 inventory turns a day for a USD 12K operator at the upper margin [inf]. A study of 3.5M cross-chain intents found deBridge had "higher solver profitability" without giving small-solver figures ([arXiv 2602.17805](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17805)). UniswapX filler addresses fell from more than 2,000 to 12 within months ([arXiv 2403.02525](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02525v1)). On L2s, arbitrage is only **0.03–0.05% of pool volume** on Arbitrum, Base and Optimism ([arXiv 2406.02172](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02172v2)). On Base, "probabilistic" bots produce 95% of spam and use 20% of gas ([Flashbots](https://collective.flashbots.net/t/to-wait-or-to-probe-arbitrage-competition-on-high-throughput-blockchains/5759)). A widely quoted "82% of Base MEV bots are profitable" comes from a paper whose reviewer flagged inconsistencies, and "profitable" there means profitable at all, not at scale ([Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2607.24172)). Solana logged 90.4M arbitrages averaging **USD 1.58** ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)), so USD 10–15K a year means winning 6,300–9,500 of them against co-located searchers [inf]. Jito's revenue meanwhile fell for five straight quarters ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-q2-2026-protocol-revenue-falls-45-to-128m-as-bam-reaches-33-of-solana-stake)).

Exchange-funded income fails on arithmetic before competition even enters. Hyperliquid pays maker rebates only above 0.5% of exchange-wide maker volume ([Dwellir](https://www.dwellir.com/blog/hyperliquid-fees-explained)). One guide puts that threshold at about USD 385M of maker fills per 14 days, and says USD 20M of monthly fills at the first tier earns about USD 200 ([Hyperliquid guide](https://hyperliquidguide.com/guides/trading/hyperliquid-market-making-guide)). dYdX's −1.1 bp rebate needs more than USD 125M of 30-day volume plus a 4% market share ([dYdX docs](https://docs.dydx.xyz/concepts/trading/rewards)). Paradex gives API orders no rebate at all ([Paradex](https://docs.paradex.trade/trading/trading-fees)). Even a −0.25 bp rebate with no share gate needs about USD 1.6M of maker fills a day to earn USD 15K a year [inf]. The repo's thin-venue market-making test (#322) already failed ([PREREG_SWEEP3](../docs/PREREG_SWEEP3_20261122.md)). Rebates lower the cost of an edge that already exists. They are not income on their own.

## Malaysian promotions and DePIN hardware stop at tens of percent

Malaysia's own "someone else pays" offers are legal, verifiable and small. IPO ballots are the closest local analogue to prop recycling because they renew 55–60 times a year and lock capital only from application to refund: Bursa had 60 IPOs in 2025 ([NST](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/corporate/2025/12/1346105/malaysia-ipos-add-nearly-rm33bil-market-cap-2025-closes)), and the hottest 2025 ACE Market deal, THMY, was 35.6× oversubscribed and opened 158% above its IPO price ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2025/10/13/thmy-ipo-oversubscribed-3557-times-ahead-of-ace-market-debut)). The repo's pre-registered test on 19 IPOs found a mean value of only **0.40% per ringgit applied**, positive in the first half (+1.69%) and negative in the second (−0.76%), with 37% closing below issue, so it FAILED. Measured after the fact, it was RM 7.7–17K a year (about USD 1.9–4.3K), with a bootstrap 90% range of RM 2–31K ([PREREG_LOOPHOLES](../docs/PREREG_LOOPHOLES_20261120.md)). Stacking every promotion found gives roughly **RM 5–25K in year one (USD 1.3–6K, about 10–40% on RM 60K)**, with the upper end driven almost entirely by the IPO lottery [inf]. The pieces are a one-off broker welcome pack, 0% balance-transfer float parked at about 4% (about RM 1,200 on RM 30,000), i-Saraan, PRS tax relief and capped cashback. The parking rates underneath are 2.75% OPR ([BNM](https://www.bnm.gov.my/-/monetary-policy-statement-05112026)), fixed-deposit promotions of about 4% ([RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/fixed-deposits/best-fixed-deposit-accounts-in-malaysia.html)) and EPF's 6.15% ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/02/28/epf-announces-615pc-dividend-for-both-conventional-shariah-savings-for-2025/210707)). For liquidations nothing new is needed except patience and two decisions only Jx can make. The registered shadow verdict on 2026-12-19 decides whether a simple bot beats the incumbents often enough ([PREREG_LIQ_SHADOW](../docs/PREREG_LIQ_SHADOW_20261121.md)). Because income clusters in crashes, the log-only extension to mid-2027 matters more than the four-week number. The remaining speed question needs a free-tier RPC key for a Base flashblocks endpoint, and any VPS or live gas money needs Jx's explicit approval ([NOTES_JX](../docs/NOTES_JX.md)). For points farming, the new pre-registration must use a different method from #360, which stays concluded as "not measurable". The method is pool ÷ volume: weekly pool value r = weekly points × tokens per point × claim-day price, divided by the venue's weekly eligible volume, compared against hedge cost c (fees on both legs, spread, funding difference). Assuming USD 10–15M a year of hedged volume is feasible on USD 10K (my assumption), USD 15K a year needs about 1–1.5 bp of net rebate. At Hyperliquid-like fees, the pool must therefore pay roughly 4–11 bp per dollar of volume gross [inf]. Live programmes can also be logged forward week by week and valued at their launch.

For CoW, the per-solver payout distribution answers in one pull whether the long tail earns the target before any engineering starts. If it survives, CoW's shadow competition lets an own solver bid on live orders without settling or risking money, which is the cleanest newcomer test of any candidate ([CoW docs](https://cowswap.mintlify.app/cow-protocol/howto/solvers/onboard); [CoW API](https://cowswap.mintlify.app/api-reference/get-information-about-a-solver-competition)). For intent filling, per-filler income = input − output − gas can be reconstructed from fill events, following the method of the 3.5M-intent study ([arXiv 2602.17805](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17805)). The time-to-fill distribution shows directly whether a slower filler ever sees an order. The L2 arbitrage pilot reuses the liquidation replay's block-level method. Its kill threshold is deliberately loose (USD 300 a month against a USD 1,250 decision number), so noise cannot kill a real opportunity. Each pilot costs hours of GitHub Actions time and no money. Every run must be re-run once for identical output and get a row in `docs/FIGURES_LEDGER.md`.

## Conclusion

The search question needs reframing. Asking "which strategy returns more than 100%" leads straight to crowded races. The better question is "which third party pays a non-rival, renewable subsidy to anyone who meets a rule". Under that lens the evidence sorts cleanly. Rival pools (liquidations, solver rewards, points pools, MEV, maker rebates) become winner-take-most within months, and the slices left to a newcomer are small by construction, because anything worth USD 15K a year attracts a faster specialist. Non-rival subsidies (bank promotions, government matching, IPO ballots) are capped per person and mostly one-off. Prop-firm evaluations are the rare structure that is both non-rival and renewable, and the scalable path is still more Malaysia-eligible prop slots, not a non-prop replacement.

If any non-prop route breaks this pattern, it will be because a newcomer can show early that it captures a slice, not because the pie is large. The pie sizes are already known: USD 1–2M a month for liquidations outside the top three, about USD 3.7M a year for CoW solvers, and weekly points pools in the millions. What is missing everywhere is the newcomer's capture rate. The liquidation shadow log and CoW's shadow competition measure exactly that, at zero cost. Those two results, plus a survivorship-complete r − c table for points programmes, are enough to close the non-prop search with a definite answer rather than an open list.
