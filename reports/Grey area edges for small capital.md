# Most grey loopholes pay hundreds, not thousands

No mechanism found has verified evidence that one honest Malaysian resident with USD 10,000–15,000 can earn USD 15,000–20,000 a year from a grey-area edge in 2025–2026. By grey-area we mean legal, inside the platform's written rules, and disliked by the people who pay for it. The strongest candidate is the one Jx already runs. **Prop-firm rule asymmetries** mean choosing firms whose written rules suit a bot, buying evaluations during sales, and using fee refunds. They pay because failing customers fund the payouts. The repo's corrected backtest gives **USD 3,689 a year per Topstep account path**. The multi-firm plan's **USD 18,006 a year on USD 13,858 at risk** was modelled before the open-P&L correction, so it is now an upper bound. Second, and the only other candidate with tested returns above 100% a year, is not a classic loophole. It is the repo's own **resting bids under forced-liquidation wicks** on Hyperliquid. In the 2025–26 out-of-sample backtest it made **USD 26,680 a year on a USD 15,000 pot**. That figure was chosen after many variants were tried on the same window, it has never traded live, and the registered forward test only starts on 2026-12-01. Everything that looks most like a classic loophole stays under about USD 4,000 a year per person. That covers odd-lot tenders, reverse-split round-ups, one-lot IPO ballots, Malaysian card and bank promotions, cashback stacking, ticket and sneaker resale, and domain drop-catching. These edges are capped per identity, and lifting the cap would need extra identities, which is excluded. The crypto edges that do clear the target belong to specialist teams. Backrunning, JIT liquidity, launch sniping and points farming are races where the speed tier or insiders take most of the money. The pattern is structural: whoever pays for a loophole either caps it (one per person), races it away (one block per winner) or bans it in writing once it costs enough. This report cannot prove that no edge exists. It covers mechanisms with a public footprint, and many external figures came from search-result snippets because the sandbox blocked most primary pages.

## Whoever pays for a loophole caps it, races it away, or bans it

A grey-area edge always has a payer: an issuer, a platform's marketing budget, liquidity providers, liquidated traders or failing customers. The payer's response decides whether a small player can keep the edge. In the 2025–2026 evidence every payer had one of three responses, and each one limits a small player.

The first response is a **per-person cap**, and it is why the TradFi and retail quirks are legal and small. Issuers that give odd-lot holders priority in a Dutch-auction tender buy at most 99 shares per holder, and commissions "can swamp the odd-lot return on 99 shares" ([helpyourfinances](https://helpyourfinances.com/risk-behind-odd-lot-tender-offers-my-fdx-experience-explained/)). A reverse-split round-up pays one extra share's worth per account. Hong Kong IPO one-lot ballots pay one lottery ticket per identity. Malaysian cashback cards cap rewards at **RM10–110 a month per card or category** ([RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/credit-card-news/rhb-shell-visa-cashback-update.html); [CompareHero](https://www.comparehero.my/credit-card/cashback/page-4)). More capital does not lift any of these caps. Lifting them would need more identities, which is excluded.

The second response is a **race**, which is how the payer's money gets split when anyone may take part. Where good data exists, the race goes to a few integrated players. On Ethereum, CEX-DEX arbitrage worth an estimated **USD 233.8M from August 2023 to March 2025 went to 19 major searchers**. Searchers tied exclusively to one block builder passed most of their revenue to that builder, "sometimes even operating at negative net profit" ([Wu et al., AFT 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol354-aft2025/html/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.26/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.26.html)). On BNB Smart Chain in 2025, two builders produced over 96% of blocks and took about 92% of MEV profit ([arXiv 2602.15395](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15395)). On Base, the high-frequency version of one backrun strategy earned **1,682.92 ETH against 16.13 ETH** for the plain version ([arXiv 2609.27424](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27424v1)). Over half of Base block gas in Q1 2025 was spam probes, and only 6–12% of them led to a trade ([arXiv 2604.00234](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00234)). A newcomer in a race does not get a share of a new pie. It only dilutes an existing one, and it pays gas on every probe that loses.

The third response is a **written ban**, which arrives once an edge costs the payer enough. Prop firms now ban in writing:

- hedging across accounts or firms ([Lucid](https://support.lucidtrading.com/en/articles/11404734-prohibited-hedging); [FundedNext](https://fundednext.com/futures-challenge-terms));
- copying other people's signals ([FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/futures/forbidden-trading-practices/));
- latency arbitrage and emulators ([The Trading Pit](https://support.thetradingpit.com/en/articles/835084-cfd-prime-swing-rules));
- news-error scalping ([HyroTrader](https://www.hyrotrader.com/trading-rules/));
- microscalping, flagged at Lucid when more than half of profit comes from trades held 5 seconds or less ([Lucid](https://support.lucidtrading.com/en/articles/11404742-prohibited-microscalping)).

Retail CFD brokers void latency-arbitrage profits "without prior notice" ([Collyer Bristow on FXCM](https://collyerbristow.com/?p=5752)). The bans also reach TradFi. In March 2025 SIFMA asked the exchanges to replace reverse-split round-ups with cash, calling round-up buying "a novel trading practice designed to profit from the 'round-up'" ([SIFMA letter](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/SIFMA-Reverse-Split-and-Fractional-Shares-Letter-to-Exchanges-03.25.25.pdf), read via search summary). Hong Kong's August 2025 IPO reform cut one-lot success rates, and the first IPO under the new rules allotted shares to only **18,279 of 256,072 applicants (7.1%)** ([China Daily HK](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/hk/article/617926)).

Survivorship explains why the anecdotes look better than the averages. An analysis of more than 300,000 prop accounts found that **14% pass and 7% ever reach a payout** ([Finance Magnates via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/financemagnates%3A3a251e333094b%3A0-exclusive-only-7-of-300-000-prop-trading-accounts-achieved-payouts/), snippet). Goat Funded Trader paid USD 26M to **22,279 of 1.2M traders, about USD 1,167 each** ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/prop-firm-goat-funded-trader-says-it-paid-26-million-to-22279-of-12-million-traders/)). Of 118 token launches in 2025, 84.7% traded below their launch valuation ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/crypto-airdrops-token-failure-utility-distribution)). In nine major airdrops, recipients sold up to 95% of their tokens soon after receiving them ([Messias et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02752v4)). The winners post their payout certificates and the losers stay silent, which inflates any count of "people who made USD 20K".

The prop subsidy differs in one respect that matters here. A faster bot elsewhere does not shrink the payout on your account, and a failed account can be bought again. The repo's coin-flip test shows the rules themselves pay: random-sign trades of the same size have a positive median at every approved firm ([START_HERE](../docs/START_HERE.md)). This report found no other grey mechanism that is non-rival, renewable and uncapped per person.

## Ten candidates ranked; two show tested returns above 100%

Each candidate is scored 1–3 on three factors, and the scores are multiplied (maximum 27). Ties are broken first by evidence and then by the best-evidenced dollars for a small player.

**E, evidence of reaching about USD 15–20K a year on small capital**, is scored against dollars, not percentages. Flash-loan strategies need almost no capital and would pass a percentage test trivially.

| E score | Meaning |
|---|---|

| 2 | The pool and the arithmetic allow the target, or incumbents reach it, but there is no newcomer or out-of-sample evidence |
| 1 | The evidence points well below the target |

| T score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | Free on-chain or official data, ideally already in the repo's pipeline |
| 2 | Partial or hand-collected data |

### Prop-firm asymmetries are the one grey edge already working

Rank 1 is mostly the benchmark seen from a different angle. The incremental question is how much the grey levers add on top of it.

**How the loss limit is measured matters most.** Topstep checks its loss limit on open P&L in real time. Correcting the repo's model for this lowered Topstep 50K to **USD 39,949 over 2016–26 (USD 3,689 a year)**, with a coin-flip median of USD 7,699 ([AUDIT_PRE_LIVE](../docs/AUDIT_PRE_LIVE_20261126.md)). Lucid's EOD drawdown ignores open P&L, and LucidFlex 50K qualifies at **USD 40,545** on Lucid's own stated rule ([START_HERE](../docs/START_HERE.md)). The same audit treats DayTraders and TTP Prime as not qualifying until their open-P&L rule is confirmed. Two of the four firms behind the USD 18,006-a-year Malaysia-eligible portfolio are therefore in doubt ([PREREG_PORTFOLIO_MY](../docs/PREREG_PORTFOLIO_MY_20261123.md)). The corrected multi-firm figure has not been computed.

**Copy permissions decide how many accounts one bot can run.** DayTraders is the only firm found that permits copying into accounts at other firms in writing. FFN counts identical bots across accounts as copy trading and caps them at five funded accounts ([FFN](https://app.fundedfuturesnetwork.com/nexus/rules/article-7-1-copy-trading-limits-and-rules)).

**Promotions lower the fee side.** Third-party trackers show Apex ran codes of up to 90% off roughly every one to two months through 2025–26 ([invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2026/05/28/apex-trader-funding-launches-90-off-promotion-5-pack-bundles-zero-fee-options/)). In late November 2026 the official pages showed FFN at 70% off and Phidias at up to 85% off ([FFN](https://fundedfuturesnetwork.com/accounts); [Phidias](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/faq)). Topstep's discounts are smaller: 15% by referral, and a code covers a new Combine or a reset, not both ([Topstep](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/10513413-getting-started-with-the-topstep-dashboard)).

**Fee refunds on the first payout** exist mainly at CFD-style firms: FTMO, FXIFY, AquaFunded, and TTP Prime ([FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/); [FXIFY](https://fxify.com/programs/three-phase-challenge/); [AquaFunded](https://aquafunded.com/)). They make an evaluation free if it passes.

The benefit of a promotion depends on how the firm charges. A discount on a one-time fee divides the break-even pass rate by the same factor. On a monthly subscription, the same discount saves only about d/M of the cost, where M is the number of months the bot needs to pass [inf]. The repo should therefore treat promotions as a cost-of-acquisition lever on one-time-fee products, not as a new income source.

Two warnings attach to this rank. First, **several firms ban another person's strategy**. FTMO forbids trades that "replicate, follow, or otherwise incorporate the trading decisions of any third party" ([FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/futures/forbidden-trading-practices/)). Blueberry allows a bot only if "you built it yourself" ([Blueberry](http://help.blueberryfutures.com/en/articles/12921692-can-i-use-eas-bots-or-copy-trading)). DayTraders bans "trading for other people". The bot is the friend's strategy, so these clauses matter for any account in Jx's name. The notes found no explicit Topstep or Lucid wording either way. Second, **denials cluster around coordinated or identical trading and VPS use**. Those are exactly the patterns a one-bot, multi-account plan creates, so the plan is safe only at firms that permit it in writing ([edge-ledger](https://edge-ledger.io/prop-firm-payout-tracker), anecdotal).

### Wick bids are the only non-prop result above 100%, but not yet live

The repo's wick family places resting limit bids far below the market on Hyperliquid perps and sells into the rebound. It buys from forced liquidations, so the liquidated trader pays. **#389** placed bids 15% under the previous close. It made +2.61% per fill on Hyperliquid in 2023–26 (about USD 18.8K a year on USD 15K). On Binance 2020–26, out of sample, it made +1.48% per fill (t 15, positive in 6 of 6 years) ([CANDIDATES](../docs/CANDIDATES.md)). **#529** combines #389 with a volatility-scaled, next-day-exit variant. It made **USD 26,680 a year on a USD 15K pot out of sample in 2025–26, with a worst month of −USD 417** ([NOTES_NONPROP](../docs/NOTES_NONPROP.md)).

The record is honest about the weaknesses. The edge per fill for #389 fell from about 4–5% (2023–24) to 0.9% (2025) and 2.4% (2026). The family **loses in crash regimes**: the Binance path lost USD 22K from September 2025 to July 2026. Many wick variants were judged on the same 2025–26 window. And if Hyperliquid locks margin against resting orders, the realistic figure falls to about USD 6.4K a year ([START_HERE](../docs/START_HERE.md)). The edge is "grey" only in that it profits from other traders' forced liquidations. The mechanism itself is ordinary limit-order liquidity. On the scoring rule it earns E = 3, because it is an out-of-sample, repo-verified result above the target. It is not a documented live outcome, and the forward paper test is the real check.

### The crypto races have large pools and unmeasured newcomer capture

Rank 3 has the largest single-team figure found. A HyperEVM bot team reported about **USD 5M of profit over about 8 months** arbitraging HYPE between HyperEVM pools and Hyperliquid's order book. The edge came from 2-second price updates. A per-block gas cap limited it to about 7–8 arbitrages per block, and it got tighter as more DEXs launched ([TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/28622); [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5206943)). This is a self-report republished by crypto media, and there is no 2026 data. The Base evidence shows what happens to late entrants: the speed tier takes about 99% ([arXiv 2609.27424](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27424v1)). The repo's liquidation replays found the same thing in another race. **64–78% of Aave-fork liquidations land in the same block as the price update that triggers them**, so a perfectly fast block-level watcher reaches only about 13% of events and 10% of value ([PREREG_LIQ_REPLAY](../docs/PREREG_LIQ_REPLAY_20261121.md)). The current liquidation estimate is **USD 0–100 a month** ([START_HERE](../docs/START_HERE.md)). JIT liquidity is smaller still. The 2023 Imperial College study found an average JIT ROI of **0.007% per attack** ([IACR 2023/973](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/973.pdf)). A 2025 AFT model finds observed JIT well short of optimal, which leaves room for better JIT bots, not for small ones ([AFT 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol354-aft2025/html/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.8/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.8.html)).

Points programmes and launchpads are the only crypto items with returns above 100% for single, non-sybil wallets, and those returns came in episodes. Ethena's first season paid an estimated +26% for plain holding and up to about +103% for boosted LP positions in six weeks. This is arithmetic on third-party shard rates and a conservative launch price ([AirdropAlert](https://airdropalert.com/airdrops/ethena/); [Bitpinas](https://bitpinas.com/learn-how-to-guides/ethena-airdrop-protocol/)). The systematic data cut the other way. **Only 8 of 62 airdrops in 2024 were positive after 90 days** ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/airdrops-in-the-barren-desert/)). PANews called 2025 farming returns "far from ideal", as insider allocations grew ([PANews via HTX](https://www.htx.com/news/panews-crypto-year-end-review-did-your-2025-airdrop-harvesti-3MBKp1do/)). The repo's points-venue funding test (#534) halved from 14.6% to 4.1% a year on capital across its two periods ([CANDIDATES](../docs/CANDIDATES.md)). Virtuals Genesis peaks of ×4 to ×128 (average ×32) come from promotional coverage of about 24 launches to May 2025. Later only about 6 of 23 launches "pulled ahead" ([TechFlow](https://techflowpost.com/en-US/article/25779); [AltcoinBuzz](https://www.altcoinbuzz.io/cryptocurrency-news/top-15-virtuals-protocol-genesis-launches-by-point-value/)). Binance Alpha averaged **USD 1,076 gross per user** across 2025 ([CMC](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/uk/article/binance-alpha-2025-crypto-airdrops-token-launches)). That is before volume costs of roughly USD 10–20 for every 15 points ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/the-race-for-alpha-points-a-trap-for-airdrop-hunters-or-binances-gold-mine/)), and Binance sits on the SC alert list.

## Identity-capped TradFi and retail quirks stay under USD 4,000 a year

The TradFi edges that critics call aggressive are the clearest grey-area cases, and they are reliably small. The repo's pre-registered test of Hong Kong one-lot applications covered 301 IPOs from January 2024 to October 2026. The average first-day return was +35.6%, and the median one-lot success rate was 7%. The strategy made **−USD 277 a year with HK$100 fees and +USD 1,092 a year with a zero-fee broker** ([CANDIDATES](../docs/CANDIDATES.md) #391). Hot IPOs (oversubscribed 100× or more) gained 53% on day one, but their median one-lot success rate was 3%, so the allocation odds were already priced in. IPO margin used to be the aggressive variant: Mixue drew about HK$1.84 trillion of retail margin ([The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/70503/Retail-investors-pump-record-184tn-into-IPO)). Margin scales pool size, not odds, and the SFC reinstated a minimum 10% deposit in March 2025 ([The Standard](https://thestandard.com.hk/section-news/section/11/271136/Cap-on-IPO-margin-loans-revived-to-ease-demand)).

The US issuer-funded edges are shrinking as well. Reverse-split round-ups still appear in some 2025–26 filings, such as Healthcare Triangle's 2026 proxy ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839285/000121390026005400/ea0273071-def14a_health.htm)). More 2026 issuers pay cash in lieu, as Surf Air and Cardlytics do ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001936224/000114036126023346/ny20063944x1_pre14a.htm)). Others leave rounding "at the participant level", so a broker may not pass the extra share through. Odd-lot tenders range from WEX's full fill of odd lots ([dombey.capital](https://www.dombey.capital/blog/what-is-a-dutch-auction-tender-offer-the-complete-guide)) to MiniLuxe, which bought no shares at all (search summary of a blocked substack). Stacked optimistically, a Malaysian resident might make **USD 2–4K a year** from zero-fee HK IPOs, odd-lot tenders, round-ups and one-off broker promotions together [inf]. Moomoo MY and Webull MY sign-up rewards run at RM200–500 per broker per campaign ([moomoo MY](https://www.moomoo.com/my/support/topic9_565)). The investor-side legality is clear: BNM allows foreign investment of any amount without domestic borrowing, and foreign-sourced dividends are exempt for individuals until 2036 ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/my/en/assets/publications/tax-in-motion/2025/pwc-tax-in-motion-budget2026-edition.pdf)). What is not verified is whether Malaysian broker entities give access to HK or US IPO books and pass round-ups through.

Malaysian retail and fintech edges are capped even more tightly. About four cashback cards, a few sign-up gifts (RM125–150 each through aggregators), digital-bank bonus rates of 3–4% and occasional remittance referrals together come to roughly **USD 700–1,900 a year** [inf on official caps]. Touch 'n Go treats manual credit-card reloads above RM1,000 a month as transferable balance ([SoyaCincau](https://soyacincau.com/2023/10/15/touch-n-go-ewallet-fee-manual-credit-card-reload-rm1000-per-month/)), and campaign terms commonly exclude reloads, which closes the manufactured-spend route. Online arbitrage could in principle exceed 100% on capital through turnover. But USD 18K a year at a 10% margin needs about USD 180K of sales, roughly 3,000–4,000 orders. That is a full-time logistics job, and its evidence base is estimates such as the 5–15% margins in [SellerAssistant](https://www.sellerassistant.app/blog/amazon-profit-margin-for-sellers/) rather than measured outcomes [inf]. Ticket scalping is legal today, but KPDN is drafting a definition of scalping, bulk-purchase limits and name/ID tickets under Act 723 ([Malay Mail](https://malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/13/minister-anti-scalping-provisions-to-be-added-to-price-control-act/238944)). None of these reaches even the single-account Topstep figure once labour is costed.

## Conclusion

The grey-area framing does not open a new source of income. Loopholes that are legal and within the rules are small precisely because the payer can cap them per person, race them away or ban them. The ones that pay well are the ones that break a rule, which is why they appear in the exclusion table. Two results survive, and neither is a loophole in the usual sense. In both, the payer is a crowd of losing traders that a written rule or ordinary market structure exposes: prop customers who fail evaluations, and leveraged traders who get liquidated. The useful search target is therefore not a cleverer trick. It is another place where many people predictably lose under fixed rules, which a patient, rule-abiding bot can stand on the other side of without racing anyone.

Three decisions follow from this. The prop plan's real uncertainty is no longer the strategy but the paperwork: open-P&L rules, third-party-bot clauses and payout caps at the specific Malaysia-eligible firms. Those checks are worth more than any new grey idea. The wick forward test, which reports by 2027-01-26, is the single result most likely to change the plan. Until it reports, the USD 26.7K backtest should be treated as a candidate, not income. HyperEVM arbitrage and JIT need one free pilot each, and they should be expected to fail.
