FAQ Questions about the Pro plan.
What's the difference between Pro and Rapid? Rapid is available in four sizes ($25K–$150K) and pays daily on sim with a 90/10 split using intraday trailing drawdown. Pro starts at $50K, goes up to $150K, pays on a 14-day cycle with a $100,000 cap, uses EOD trailing drawdown, and has a defined path to live capital. Rapid is built for speed and cadence. Pro is built for scale and the live transition.
How does the Pro live transition actually work? There are three ways a Pro account can transition to live. The most common is three consecutive payouts on the same account — after which the account is reviewed by our Risk Team. The second is profits exceeding the $100,000 payout cap, where the excess is transitioned to a live balance, up to the maximum allowed by the account size, rather than paid out. The third is a proactive outreach from the Risk Team at any point during your sim funded journey based on your performance. Approved traders move to a real brokerage account at Blue Row Capital — real money, daily payouts, and an 80/20 split. Note: live account parameters differ from sim funded, including a daily loss limit, reduced contract limits, and a static drawdown floor. The trigger is documented; the review is human.
Why is there a 14-day payout window on Pro? The 14-day window runs from your first trade on the sim funded account. It gives the EOD trailing drawdown time to settle toward a stable floor and establishes an equity track record before your first payout request. You also need to have cleared the required buffer before a payout can be approved — so the practical unlock is whichever comes later: 14 calendar days or buffer cleared.
What's the max payout I can actually withdraw on Pro per cycle? Up to $100,000 per cycle, subject to your live equity above the required buffer at the time of request. Most competing "pro" tiers cap each cycle at $5,000 or 50% of account balance — Pro is the cap that doesn't become the binding constraint in your best months.
What is Blue Row Capital and how do I get there? Blue Row Capital is the live brokerage relationship MFFU traders graduate into. Once your Pro account hits three consecutive payouts and clears risk-team review, your account is transitioned to a Blue Row Capital live brokerage seat — same rules, real money, real broker.
Is the Pro plan worth the cost over the Rapid plan? It's worth it if you're optimizing for scale over cadence. Rapid is the better economic choice for active traders who want daily cashflow at a 90/10 split on smaller sizes. Pro is the better choice if you want a single account with up to $100K per payout, $50K–$150K sizes, no daily loss limit once funded, and a defined live transition.
How is the drawdown calculated on Pro? Pro uses end-of-day trailing drawdown in both eval and sim funded. The max-loss line trails your equity high at the end of each trading day — not intraday. After your first approved payout, the MLL locks permanently at your starting balance plus $100 and stops trailing entirely. Until that first payout, the MLL continues to trail each time you set a new EOD equity high.
Does the Pro plan have a consistency rule? There's a 50% consistency rule in evaluation only — no single day's profit can exceed 50% of your total eval profits. Once you're funded, there is no hard consistency rule. That said, the Pro plan does carry a Consistent Trading Policy: MFFU monitors sim funded accounts for trading behavior that falls outside professional norms — things like maxing contracts on every trade, contract flipping, inconsistent position sizing, or high-frequency strategies not aligned with your stated approach. Violations can result in restrictions or account termination. Trade like a professional and it won't be an issue.
What's the difference between EOD trailing and intraday trailing drawdown? Intraday trailing (used by Rapid) updates the trailing max-loss line in real time as your equity moves up during the session — your highest unrealized P&L can pull the floor up against you. EOD trailing (used by Pro) only updates the line at the end of each trading day, based on your closing balance. EOD is the model used at most live brokerage seats, and it's more forgiving of mid-session volatility.
