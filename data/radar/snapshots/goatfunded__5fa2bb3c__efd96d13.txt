Is hedging allowed?
No, hedging is strictly prohibited. This includes:
Hedging within the same trading account.
Hedging between multiple accounts owned by the same trader.
Hedging between accounts owned by different traders.
Hedging between a GoatFundedTrader (GFT) account another Firm’s account, either if the two accounts are owned by the same person or by two different people.
Hedging is defined as opening two opposite positions on the same asset, regardless of lot size or calculated risk. This rule is in place to maintain fair and transparent trading practices and to protect the integrity of our trading environment.
If we detect any hedging activity—especially between a GFT account and an external firm—the account will be breached immediately, and the user will be permanently banned from our platform. In the case of a Simulated Funded Account, the trader will not be eligible for any rewards.
Can I use Expert Advisors (EAs)?
You can use an Expert Advisor only if you developed it yourself. Third-party EAs are not allowed at any time. This includes purchased, shared and off-the-shelf EAs, and any EA marketed to pass evaluations. Our team has the right to request proof of ownership at any time, for example the source code. If you cannot provide it, the account is treated as in breach.
High-frequency trading (HFT) systems and Gold Arbitrage EAs are not allowed in any case, and your EA must still follow the prohibited trading practices rule.
Trade copier EAs count as third-party EAs, so they are not allowed either. To copy trades between your Goat Funded Trader accounts, use the GOAT Trade Copier in the Trader Dashboard. See Copy trading and signals .
Note: From October 6, 2026, VPNs, VPSs, proxies and any other hosting service are not allowed during trading on any account. If your EA needs a VPS to run, you cannot use it on a Goat Funded Trader account. See Can I use a VPN, VPS, proxy, or any other hosting service while trading?

What is the minimum holding time for trades?
For funded accounts, any profit from a trade that is open for less than the minimum holding time is considered invalid and is removed when a payout is requested.
The minimum holding time depends on when the account was purchased:
Purchased before 6 October 2026 (00:00 UTC): 2 minutes (120 seconds)
Purchased from 6 October 2026 (00:00 UTC) onward: 1 minute (60 seconds)
A funded account follows the purchase date of the challenge it came from. If you reset a challenge that was purchased before 6 October 2026, it stays at 2 minutes, even if the reset happens after that date.
Any losses from trades closed before the minimum holding time remain and are the trader's responsibility.
This means you may open and close positions faster than the minimum holding time if you wish. Profits from those trades are not included in payout calculations, while losses still count.
Having profits removed for this reason is not a breach or violation of funded account rules. It only affects the payout amount.
This rule applies only to funded accounts and does not affect evaluation (challenge) accounts.
This policy helps ensure fair trading conditions and aligns with the liquidity and risk management standards required for consistent and sustainable trading across all funded traders.

You choose one lead account . This is the account you trade. Then you tick every account that should follow it.
Each follower has its own sizing, its own settings and its own copy log. Stopping one follower leaves the others running.
It works on MetaTrader 5, Match-Trader and TradeLocker , and it copies between them.
Each account gets its own cloud terminal. You don't install anything, and copying continues while your computer is off.
Our risk checks recognise trades copied through the GOAT Trade Copier, so they are not treated as prohibited copy trading.

How does the 80% margin rule work?
You can't use more than 80% of your account's margin at once. This article shows how the margin on a trade is worked out, how traders go over the limit without noticing, and where to check your margin before you trade.
The rule
Margin usage = margin used by all your open trades ÷ your equity × 100
Equity includes the floating profit or loss on your open trades, so this figure changes while your trades are open.
Every open trade counts. If you have three trades open at the same time, their margin is added together and checked as one total.
How much margin a trade uses
Margin for one trade = lot size × contract size × price ÷ leverage
Forex: 1 lot is 100,000 units of the first currency in the pair.
Gold (XAUUSD): 1 lot is 100 ounces.
Indices and other symbols: contract sizes differ. Open the symbol's specification in your platform to see it.
Leverage depends on the phase (see What is the account leverage? ):
Evaluation Funded
Forex 1:100 1:50
Indices 1:20 1:10
Commodities, incl. gold 1:20 1:10
Crypto 1:2 1:2
Stocks 1:5 1:5
Funded accounts start at half the evaluation leverage on forex, indices and commodities. The same trade uses twice as much margin once you're funded.
Example 1: one trade that is too big
$10,000 evaluation account. Gold is at $4,000 (a round number for the example). Leverage is 1:20.
1 lot of gold uses 100 × $4,000 ÷ 20 = $20,000 of margin.
0.30 lot uses $6,000. That's 60% of $10,000, so it's within the rule.
0.45 lot uses $9,000. That's 90%, over the rule.
Example 2: several small trades together
Same account, same price. You open three gold trades of 0.15 lot each.
Each one uses $3,000, which is 30% on its own.
Together they use $9,000, which is 90%. Over the rule.
The rule looks at the total, not at each trade.
Example 3: the same lot size after you're funded
$10,000 funded account at 1:10. Gold at $4,000.
1 lot of gold uses 100 × $4,000 ÷ 10 = $40,000 of margin.
0.25 lot uses $10,000. That's 100%. Over the rule.
In the evaluation, the same 0.25 lot used $5,000, or 50%. A lot size that was fine in your evaluation can break the rule on your funded account.
Example 4: a losing trade pushes you over
$10,000 funded account at 1:50. You open 3.9 lots of USDJPY. The dollar is the first currency in the pair, so 1 lot is $100,000.
Margin: 3.9 × $100,000 ÷ 50 = $7,800. That's 78%, within the rule.
The trade goes $300 against you. Your equity drops to $9,700.
$7,800 ÷ $9,700 = 80.4%. You're over the rule without opening anything new.
If a floating loss alone takes you over, your trades were already close to the limit when you opened them.
Where to check your margin
Trading platforms don't show margin usage. They show Margin Level, which uses the same two numbers the other way round:
Margin Level = equity ÷ margin × 100
Margin usage (our rule) Margin Level (your platform)
50% 200%
80% 125%
100% 100%
If your Margin Level is above 125%, you're under 80%.
MT5 desktop. Open the Toolbox (Ctrl+T) and go to the Trade tab. The line above your open trades shows Balance, Equity, Margin, Free Margin and Margin Level. Margin Level only appears while a trade is open.
MT5 mobile. Open the Trade tab. Balance, Equity, Margin, Free margin and Margin level are at the top.
cTrader. The TradeWatch panel at the bottom of the screen shows Balance, Equity, Margin, Free Margin and Margin Level.
What happens if you go over
Going over 80% doesn't close your trade or stop you opening new ones. Your trader dashboard shows flagged trades in the Flagged so far card, but the final amount is set when our Risk team reviews your payout.
On a funded account, our Risk team removes the profit made on flagged trades when it reviews your payout. Only profit is removed. A flagged trade that closed at a loss stays in your results. Depending on the case, our Risk team can instead move a funded account back to an evaluation phase.
On an evaluation account, nothing happens to your account while you trade. Moving from Phase 1 to Phase 2 is automatic. Our Risk team reviews flagged trades from all phases together when you pass your final phase, before your account goes live.
Risk looks at how much of your profit came from flagged trades. If it's a small amount, your pass stands. If it's a large part of your profit, Risk removes that profit and your pass isn't validated. You get a new account for your final phase (a new Phase 2 on a 2-Step, a new account on a 1-Step), and your trading days start again:
If you still have profit left after the deduction, it carries over to the new account.
If the deduction would take you below your starting balance, the new account starts at your starting balance.
Example: you pass Phase 2 of a $5,000 evaluation at $5,400. $350 of your profit came from flagged trades. Risk removes the $350 and your new Phase 2 starts at $5,050. If the flagged trades had made $500, removing it would leave you below $5,000, so your new Phase 2 would start at $5,000.

Is News Trading allowed?
Can I trade during news events with Goat Funded Trader?
Yes. Trading during news events is allowed and does not break any Goat Funded Trader rules.
Is there a limit on profit from trades around high-impact news?
Yes. From 24 November 2026 , the limit applies to each news release, not to each trade. Every trade opened or closed within 5 minutes before or after a high-impact news release (marked with a red folder on ForexFactory.com or Myfxbook.com) counts toward that release. Together, those trades have a maximum profit, and the maximum depends on when you purchased the account:
Purchased on or after 10 November 2026: 3% of the account's initial balance per news release
Purchased before 10 November 2026: 1% of the account's initial balance per news release
Which trades count toward a news release?
Every trade opened or closed between 5 minutes before and 5 minutes after the release. That includes:
A trade you opened earlier that closes during the window
A trade you open during the window
A pending order that triggers during the window
A trade closed during the window by Stop Loss, by Take Profit or manually
A trade that opens during one news release and closes during another counts toward both releases.
When is the profit counted?
Once every trade linked to the release is closed. If one of them stays open for hours, or until the next day, we wait until it closes. Then we add up the profit from those trades and compare the total with your limit.
Which limit applies to my account?
The purchase date decides it, and nothing else. Accounts purchased before 10 November 2026 keep the 1% limit. They do not move to 3%.
A funded account follows the purchase date of the challenge it came from. If you bought the challenge before 10 November 2026 and pass after that date, your funded account stays at 1%. If you reset an account that was purchased before 10 November 2026, it also stays at 1%, even if the reset happens after that date.
Does this policy apply to both the challenge and funded phases?
Yes. Both limits apply to the challenge phase and the funded phase.
What happens if the total is above the limit?
Only the profit above the limit is removed. You keep the profit up to the limit.
Example on a $100,000 account: a trade you opened earlier closes with $2,000 profit during a red folder release. You also open a second trade inside the window, and it closes three hours later with $2,500 profit. Both trades count toward the same release, so the total is $4,500.
Purchased on or after 10 November 2026: the limit is $3,000. You keep $3,000 and $1,500 is removed.
Purchased before 10 November 2026: the limit is $1,000. You keep $1,000 and $3,500 is removed.
This is not a breach and there is no penalty on the account. The deduction usually happens during account reviews.
What changed on 24 November 2026?
Before 24 November 2026, the limit applied to each trade separately. From 24 November 2026, it applies to the total of all trades linked to a news release. The 3% and 1% limits themselves did not change.
The new method applies to every payout request that is still under review on 24 November 2026, including requests submitted before that date. Payout requests already decided before 24 November 2026 stay as they are.

What Time Does A Trading Day Start?
Trading days are calculated based on 5 PM EST.
A new trading day starts at 5:00:01 PM EST and runs until 4:59:59 PM EST the next day.
A trade counts toward the trading day in which it is closed. A trade closed after 5 PM EST counts toward the next day's trading day.
For example:
A trade closed on Monday at 4 PM EST counts toward Monday's trading day.
A trade closed on Monday at 6 PM EST counts toward Tuesday's trading day.
This timing aligns with the global forex market session rollover, ensuring consistency across all trader accounts.
What is considered a Trading Day?
A trading day runs from 5 PM EST to 5 PM EST.
A day is counted as a trading day when you close at least one position during it and the minimum daily profit requirement for your model and phase is met. Opening a trade without closing it does not count.
Where a profit requirement applies, the day must close with a profit of at least 0.5% of the initial account balance, net of fees and commissions. These days are called minimum profitable days.
The number of days required for each model is listed in What are the Minimum Profitable Days?

What Is the Consistency Rule?
Why Do We Have a Consistency Rule?
The Consistency Rule is designed to encourage steady and sustainable trading performance. It helps ensure that traders are not relying on one unusually large trading day.
What Is the Consistency Rule?
The Consistency Rule means that no single trading day can account for X% or more of your total profits during the payout period.
The consistency percentage may vary depending on the challenge or funding model.
You can check whether your challenge has a Consistency Rule in our detailed model FAQs: https://help.goatfundedtrader.com/en/collections/11811982-models
Example
For example, if the Consistency Rule is 15% and your highest profit day should not exceed 15% of the total profit earned.
If your total profit earned is $5000, your highest trading day should not exceed (5000 X 15% ) = $750.
What Happens If I Do Not Meet the Consistency Rule?
Not meeting the Consistency Rule will not result in account termination or an account breach. However, you will not be able to request a payout until your highest profit day is below X% of your total profits for the payout period.
What Should I Do Next?
You should continue trading and generating profits until your highest profit day represents less than X% of your total profits.
Keep in mind that in order to decrease the consistency %, your max profit should be less than your current highest trading day.
How Are Partial Trades Counted for the Consistency Rule?
Each partial close counts on the trading day it happens. If you close one position in several parts on different days, the profit or loss from each part goes to the day that part was closed, and the consistency check uses those daily figures. No single day carries the whole trade.
Example of Partial Trade Calculation
You open one large trade and close a third on Monday, a third on Tuesday and the rest on Wednesday. Monday's part counts toward Monday's profit, Tuesday's toward Tuesday, Wednesday's toward Wednesday.
Trading days run 5 PM to 5 PM EST, so a partial close at 6 PM EST on Monday counts toward Tuesday's trading day.

Is there any inactivity limit?
Understanding GoatFundedTrader's Inactivity Policy
If no trades are placed on your account for 30 consecutive days, it will be breached due to inactivity. This rule applies to both Evaluation and Simulated Funded Accounts. Once breached, the account cannot be reinstated, and the fee will not be refunded. Make sure to stay active to keep your account in good standing.
To ensure the account remains active, consider executing at least one trade every 29 days to provide a buffer.
Common Questions About Inactivity
Is there a minimum number of trading days requirement each month?
No, the inactivity policy is solely based on ensuring at least one trade within a 30-day period. The monthly minimum trading day requirements apply only when progressing accounts or requesting payouts.
