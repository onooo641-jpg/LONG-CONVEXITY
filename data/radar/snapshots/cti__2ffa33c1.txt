Why Drawdown Rules Make or Break Your Challenge
Many prop firm challenge failures aren’t because traders can’t hit profit targets. They’re because of drawdown violations. The difference between passing and failing often comes down to how your prop firm calculates risk, not just your trading ability.
You could have a winning swing trade setup, perfect technical analysis, and still get stopped out mid-position because your prop firm uses equity-based drawdown rules that punish you for temporary unrealized profits.
Since 2018, we’ve taken a different approach. While other firms create moving targets with equity-based calculations, we use balance-based drawdown across all funding programs. This focuses on actual losses, not market noise.
This guide explains why our balance-based system gives you more breathing room than other firms and how it can make the difference in your challenge success. We start with the two ways a firm can measure drawdown, then show the same trade under both, then walk through exactly how our rules work and how to trade inside them.
Drawdown 101: The Two Main Types
What Is Drawdown?
Drawdown represents how much your trading account drops from a peak to a low point during a losing streak. At CTI, we define drawdown as “a benchmark for the prop firm’s risk tolerance” and the key metric that determines whether you keep or lose your funded account.
Different prop firms calculate drawdowns in entirely different ways, affecting whether you pass or fail your challenge.
Balance-Based vs. Equity-Based: The Key Difference
Balance-Based Drawdown (✅ Our Choice):
Uses only closed trade results to measure drawdown.
Ignores floating (unrealized) profits and losses in calculations.
Provides consistent, predictable risk parameters that don’t shift mid-trade.
Equity-Based Drawdown (🚩 The Red Flag):
Includes unrealized profit when setting drawdown thresholds.
Creates moving targets that tighten whenever you show paper profits.
Can terminate accounts for losing unrealized profit, not actual money.
How Floating Profits Become Traps
Equity-based drawdown operates on a cruel irony: success can sabotage you. Every time your account equity reaches a new high, including unrealized profits, it adjusts your risk parameters, creating a tightening noose.
The Floating Profit Trap
Example Scenario:
Starting balance: $100,000.
Daily drawdown limit: 5% ($5,000).
You enter trades showing $8,000 unrealized profit.
Your equity-based stop-out jumps from $95,000 to $103,000. A normal pullback to $2,500 of unrealized profit is now a violation, even though you never lost a cent of closed balance.
Why We Call This a Red Flag
Our research shows that equity-based drawdown “can catch many traders off guard” and often punishes normal strategy fluctuations. Our guidance is clear: “If you see a prop firm using an equity-based drawdown, this is your red flag to avoid it.”
That is the theory. Here is what it looks like on one real trade.

The Outcome
CTI Balance-Based Trader: Continues holding through normal market volatility, with the potential to capture the full move for a profit.

Same trade, same market, same strategy, completely different outcomes based purely on drawdown calculation method.
So how does the CTI side of that table actually work? The rules are simple enough to fit on one line.
How Balance-Based Drawdown Works
The Formula That Protects You
Balance used for drawdown = Initial Balance + Net P&L from closed trades only
Stop-out level = Initial Balance minus your drawdown limit
This elegantly simple approach is what makes our system so trader-friendly. Your risk threshold is calculated using only the trades you’ve actually closed, not the temporary fluctuations of open positions that create psychological pressure.
The Protection Mechanism
Here’s where our approach shines: while your position might show a temporary $3,000 floating loss, our balance-based drawdown calculation doesn’t panic.
It understands that unrealized losses are just that, unrealized . They haven’t actually cost you money yet.
As it’s balance-based, you’re protected from being penalized for price movement against your open trade.
Real-World Example: The $100K Challenge Account
Account Setup:
Starting balance: $100,000.
Our 2-Step Challenge: 5% daily drawdown ($5,000).
Today’s daily stop-out level: $95,000 (start-of-day balance $100,000 minus $5,000). Fixed for the whole day.
The Scenario: Earlier in the session your open trade showed +$2,000 unrealized (equity $102,000). It then reverses and moves against you by -$3,000 (equity $97,000). Competitors that use equity-based daily drawdown trail the day’s equity high: $102,000 minus $5,000 moves their stop-out up to $97,000, so this dip is a breach.
With our balance-based approach, the daily stop-out level stays locked at $95,000 all day, giving your trade room to develop.
The Safety Net: Floating losses do count toward the stop-out check. If your equity (balance plus floating losses) reaches $95,000, positions are closed and the daily limit is breached. What never happens is the level moving against you mid-day.
The Result: Your trade has breathing room to work through temporary adverse movement without triggering premature violations.

The Numbers: Our Max & Daily Drawdown Rules Explained

Maximum Drawdown: 10% (Static)

Why This Works: The 10% cushion gives you substantial room for strategy development, while the 5% daily limit prevents catastrophic single-day losses.

Instant Funding: Ultimate Simplicity

Why We Chose Balance-Based Drawdown
Our decision to use balance-based drawdown across all programs stems from our core philosophy: giving traders room to succeed rather than trapping them with technicalities.
Room for Swing Traders to Breathe
Traditional prop firms effectively punish position traders. Their equity-based systems force scalping behavior, with quick profits taken to avoid drawdown adjustments. Our balance-based approach respects that quality setups need time to develop .
Perfect Alignment With Our No-Time-Limit Policy
There is no clock on CTI evaluations, and a static, balance-based drawdown is what makes that meaningful. You can hold a position for days or weeks without a shifting equity threshold forcing you out. You get time to let a setup mature, and a risk limit that does not move while you wait.
Eliminates Equity Curve “Noise”
Trading psychology research shows that obsessing over real-time equity fluctuations destroys decision-making.
By focusing on closed trades only, balance-based drawdown removes the psychological pressure of constantly shifting risk parameters.
Our Core Philosophy
Our approach reflects our belief in sustainable trader development: “We believe in giving traders the tools and flexibility they need to succeed. That means stable, predictable risk parameters that don’t change mid-trade.”
A forgiving drawdown model is not a license to trade loosely. These five habits keep you well inside the limits.
Five Risk-Management Tips to Stay Compliant
Based on our guidance to thousands of funded traders, here are the proven strategies to avoid drawdown violations:
1. Pre-Size Positions at 1% Risk
The Golden Rule: Never risk more than 1-2% of your account balance per trade.
Our Logic: “Proper position sizing is your first line of defense. It ensures no single trade can blow your account.”
Implementation:
$100K account = maximum $1,000 risk per trade.
Set stop-losses before entry based on technical levels.
Calculate position size mathematically, not emotionally.
Protection: At 1% risk you can absorb nine straight losses (9%) and still be inside the 10% maximum drawdown limit on the 2-Step Challenge.
2. Use Equity Alerts at 80% of Limit
The Early Warning System: Set platform alerts when approaching 80% of your drawdown limit.
Example for $100K Account:
10% drawdown limit = $90K stop-out.
Set alert at $92K (80% of $10K drawdown used).
Gives you a $2K buffer for rational decisions.
Psychological Benefit: Prevents panic-closing positions when you still have room to manage risk properly.
3. Pause Trading After 3R Day
The Circuit-Breaker Rule: If you lose 3× your normal daily risk, stop trading immediately.
Example:
Normal risk: $1,000 per day (1% of $100K).
Circuit-breaker: $3,000 daily loss.
Automatic trading halt to prevent a revenge trading spiral.
Our Insight: “This mimics the circuit-breaker approach used by institutional desks to prevent decision fatigue and emotional re-entry.”
4. Split Swing Entries
The Staged Entry Strategy: Scale into positions over 2-3 entries rather than full size immediately.
Implementation:
Entry 1: 40% of planned position.
Entry 2: 35% on setup confirmation.
Entry 3: 25% on final validation.
Advantage: Reduces initial floating losses that create psychological pressure, even in balance-based systems.
5. Journal Daily Balance
The Awareness Builder: Track your daily balance and distance to stop-out level every session.
Simple Template:
Starting Balance: ___
Ending Balance: ___
Distance to Stop-Out: ___
Largest Floating Loss: ___
Value: Builds conscious awareness of risk usage and prevents gradual drift toward danger zones.
FAQs
Is 6% Max Loss Too Strict?
Not when it’s balance-based. Our 6% limit on Instant Funding accounts provides the same practical trading room as a 10% equity-based limit at other firms. The key difference: your limit doesn’t shrink when you show unrealized profits.
Does Balance Reset After Scaling?
Yes. When your account scales, the drawdown limit is recalculated as the same percentage of the new starting balance. Scale from $100,000 to $150,000 on a 10% limit and your buffer grows from $10,000 to $15,000.
What If I Hold Trades Overnight?
Balance-based drawdown is perfect for overnight positions. Your stop-out level remains constant regardless of after-hours price action. However, your equity is still monitored to prevent catastrophic losses if floating losses become extreme.
Can I Recover From a Brief Violation?
No. We’re crystal clear: “All prop firms consider hitting the drawdown threshold a hard breach, even if your account later recovers. The violation usually results in disqualification the moment the limit is exceeded.”
Benefits at a Glance
Our balance-based drawdown protects you from being penalized for price movement against your open trade, giving swing traders the needed breathing room during temporary adverse moves.
Static approach prevents moving goalposts: your maximum loss threshold stays constant, unlike equity-based systems that tighten when you show unrealized profits.
No surprise stop-outs due to floating equity, enabling focused, disciplined trade planning without micromanaging every tick.
Pick the program whose limits fit your style, size every trade at 1%, and let the rules work for you rather than against you.
