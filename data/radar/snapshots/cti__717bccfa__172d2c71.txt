Got your first prop firm payout and not sure how to pay tax on it? You’re not alone. Most funded traders get paid before they’ve thought about tax at all.
This guide explains, in plain English, how funded trader payouts are usually taxed in the US and the UK, what records to keep, and how to file.
Disclaimer: This article is based on our research and is not tax advice. Tax rules change and depend on where you live, so always check with a qualified tax adviser.
Why Taxes Matter for Funded Traders
Every profit share you get from a prop firm is income, and income is taxed.
If you don’t report your funded trading profit tax correctly, you could end up paying fines, interest and penalties.
The simple fix is to put aside part of every payout for tax. That way a tax bill never catches you out.
You’re Self-Employed, Not an Employee
Most prop firms, including City Traders Imperium, work with traders as independent contractors . In simple terms, you’re self-employed.
That means no one takes tax off your payouts for you. You report them and pay the tax yourself.
If You Live in the US
Schedule C (Form 1040): This is where you report your payouts and take off your business costs.
Self-employment tax: If you make $400 or more in a year, you also pay 15.3% for Social Security and Medicare. You work this out on Schedule SE.
If You Live in the UK
Self Assessment: Register with HMRC as self-employed and add your payouts to your tax return. Register by 5 October after the end of the tax year you started in.
National Insurance: You pay 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% on anything above that. If your profits are very low, you can choose to pay a small weekly amount (Class 2) to protect your State Pension.
Costs You Can Deduct
You can take business costs off your profit before tax. These are things you pay for only because of your trading, such as software, data, challenge fees, courses and accountant fees.
💡 Quick tip : Write down every payout and every cost as it happens. It makes your tax return much easier.
How Your Payouts Are Taxed
Income tax: Your payouts count as income from your trading business, so they’re taxed at your normal income tax rate.
Not capital gains: You’re paid a share of the profit for trading the firm’s account. You’re not selling your own investments, so capital gains tax usually doesn’t apply.
What Records to Keep
Keep these three things:
Payouts: the date, amount and payment method of each one.
Receipts: for everything you spend on your trading, like charting tools, data, challenge fees and courses.
Your trader agreement: including the profit split and scaling rules.
💡Tip: A spreadsheet works fine, or a trading journal like TradeZella can import your trades for you.
How to File, Step by Step
Treat your payouts as self-employment income.
Gather your paperwork: payout records, any payout summaries your firm gives you, and your receipts.
Fill in the right forms:
US: Schedule C and Schedule SE.
UK: your Self Assessment return, including the self-employment pages.
Work out your profit: your total payouts minus your business costs.
Claim your costs: home office, courses, tools and so on.
File online: with IRS e-file in the US or HMRC online in the UK.
Pay on time: 15 April in the US, 31 January in the UK. In the US you can ask for more time to file, but not more time to pay.
Costs Most Funded Traders Can Deduct
Software and platforms: TradingView, MetaTrader and other subscriptions.
Market data: paid data and news services.
Learning: trading courses, books and seminars.
Home office: a fair share of rent, bills and internet if you trade from home.
Professional help: accountant fees and tax software.
Keep the receipts. If the tax office ever asks, you’ll need to show them.
Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them
Mixing your money: Use a separate bank account for payouts and trading costs. It keeps your records simple.
Leaving out small payouts: Every payout counts, however small. Report them all.
Registering late (UK): Register for Self Assessment by 5 October after your first tax year, or you may get a penalty.
Missing quarterly payments (US): You may need to pay tax every quarter to avoid a penalty.
Frequently Asked Questions
Why am I a contractor and not an employee?
You trade the firm’s account under a trader agreement and get a share of the profit, not a salary. That makes you self-employed, which also means you can deduct your business costs.
Are prop firm payouts taxed as capital gains?
Usually not. They’re treated as income, because you’re paid for trading the firm’s account, not for selling your own investments.
Do I need to pay tax every quarter?
In the US, yes, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year.
In the UK, you pay after the tax year ends. If your bill is big enough, you’ll also pay part of next year’s tax in advance, on 31 January and 31 July. These are called “payments on account”.
Final Thoughts
Trading funded accounts can be one of the fastest ways to grow your income, but it comes with tax to pay.
The good news is that a little planning goes a long way. Put money aside from every payout, keep your records, and when in doubt, ask an accountant in your country. Rules outside the US and UK can be very different.
