What is Equity-Based Drawdown?
Equity-based drawdown calculates your daily loss limit using the higher of your balance or equity at 00:00 UTC . This means unrealized profits are included when determining your daily drawdown limit.
Example: On a $10,000 account , if your equity at 00:00 UTC is $10,300 and your balance is $10,000 , the higher amount is $10,300. With a 4% daily drawdown , your limit is $412 , meaning your equity must not fall below $9,888 that day.
What is Trailing Drawdown?
Trailing drawdown is a moving loss limit that follows your highest account equity. As your equity increases, the drawdown limit moves up with it.
Example: On a $10,000 account with a 5% trailing drawdown , the initial limit is $9,500 . If your equity reaches $11,000 , the limit moves to $10,450 . If your equity falls below $10,450 , the account breaches the drawdown limit.
What is Static Drawdown?
Static drawdown is a fixed loss limit based on your initial account balance. The drawdown limit does not move up as your balance or equity increases.
Example: On a $10,000 account with an 8% static drawdown , your drawdown limit is $9,200 . This limit remains fixed, even if your account grows.

Trading Rules
What Are Maven’s Trading Style Rules?
Maven prohibits the following trading styles:
Excessive Scalping
All-In Trading
Gamifying the Challenge
Traders must follow responsible trading practices and comply with all applicable account rules.
What is excessive scalping?
Excessive scalping means holding more than 50% of your trades for less than 60 seconds (1 minute) .
Example of Excessive Scalping:
A trader makes 50 trades in total.
26 trades are held for less than 60 seconds.
24 trades are held for 60 seconds or longer.
26 out of 50 trades is 52%, which exceeds the permitted 50%.
This is considered excessive scalping and is not allowed.
What is All-in Trading rule?
All-in trading refers to risking an amount that could breach your daily drawdown or maximum drawdown in a single trade or setup .
All-in trading is not permitted on any Maven Challenge or Simulated Funded Account.
This includes:
No stop-loss: Opening a trade without a stop-loss and holding it until you pass or fail the challenge.
Risking too much: Setting a stop-loss that risks more than your account's allowed drawdown limit.
Layering positions: Opening multiple trades on the same instrument and in the same direction without proper risk management, or when the combined risk exceeds your allowed drawdown limit.
Examples of All-in trading
Example 1: No stop-loss
A trader opens a 1-lot EUR/USD trade without a stop-loss. The trader holds the position until the account either passes or fails the challenge.
This is considered all-in trading because the trader is relying on one trade to determine the outcome of the account.
Example 2: Risking more than the daily or maximum drawdown limit
A trader has a $10,000 Two-Step Challenge with a 4% daily drawdown limit ($400).
The trader opens a EUR/USD trade with a stop-loss that risks 6% of the account ($600).
If the stop-loss is hit, the account will exceed its drawdown limit and fail the challenge. This is considered all-in trading.
Example 3: Layering positions
A trader opens multiple EUR/USD trades in the same direction The trader does not use a stop-loss, or the combined risk of the trades exceeds the account's drawdown limit.
This is considered all-in trading.
Example 4: Multiple trades with open risk
A trader opens multiple trades across multiple assets, using stop-loss for these trades. However, the combined risk of these trades is greater than the drawdown limits.
What Is Gamifying the Challenge?
Gamifying the challenge means using opposing positions across accounts to create a near-guaranteed chance of passing , such as going long on one account and short on another.
Example: You open a buy trade on Account A and an equal sell trade on Account B at the same time. If the market moves up, Account A profits while Account B loses; if the market moves down, Account B profits while Account A loses. This is considered gamifying the challenge rather than genuine trading.
This also includes reversing positions between Maven and another prop firm to offset risk.
Trading large portions of your account on single bets with a 1:1.25 or lower risk-to-reward ratio may also be considered gamifying the challenge.
This approach is not permitted .
What Does Maven Classify as Cheating?
Maven prohibits trading practices that exploit the demo-trading environment or attempt to bypass the intended trading rules.
The following are prohibited:
High-Frequency Trading (HFT): Exploiting demo-trader inefficiencies through high-frequency strategies.
Toxic Trading / Order Flow: Strategies designed to exploit demo execution or pricing inefficiencies.
Arbitrage: Long/short arbitrage and reverse arbitrage.
Tick Scalping: Exploiting small tick-level price movements.
Server Execution Exploitation: Using server execution inefficiencies for profit.
Copy Trading: Copying trades from another individual. If detected, both accounts may be breached .
Grid/Gap Trading: Grid and gap trading strategies are prohibited across all Challenge and Simulated Funded Accounts.
Reverse/Group Hedging: Prohibited as described under Gamifying the Challenge.
Exclusive Hedging: Hedging accounts are prohibited.
Expert Advisors (EAs): EAs and automated trading are prohibited across all Maven platforms. All trades must be executed manually.
Gamifying the Challenge: Prohibited as described above, including betting solely on maximum drawdown or take-profit levels.
For rule assessment, a trade may comprise one position or multiple entries on the same pair with similar timings and lot sizes. Entries with vastly smaller lot sizes do not count as separate trades.
Your account will be terminated if you perform any of these actions.

What are the Drawdown Rules for the Standard 1 Step Challenge and Simulated Funded Account?
The Standard 1 Step Challenge and Simulated Funded Account use the same loss limits: 3% daily loss and 5% trailing maximum drawdown.
Daily Loss Limit: 3% (Equity-Based)
The daily drawdown resets based on the higher of your equity or balance at 00:00 UTC .
Your account must not exceed a 3% loss within a trading day.
Example: You have a $10,000 account , open a trade, and leave it running overnight. At 00:00 UTC , your equity is $10,300 , while your balance is $10,000 . Your maximum daily drawdown is 3% of $10,300 ($309) . Your account will breach the daily drawdown if your equity falls below $9,991 that day.
Maximum Drawdown: 5% (Trailing)
Your maximum drawdown moves up with your highest running equity , including unrealized profit from open trades.
Example: With a $10,000 account and a 5% trailing drawdown , your initial limit is $9,500 . If your running equity reaches $11,000 , the limit moves to $10,450 . Falling below $10,450 breaches the drawdown limit.
Note: The trailing drawdown is based on running equity , not just closed trades.
Exceeding either limit will result in a breach of the Challenge or Simulated Funded Account.

What is the M2 Account Saver and How Does It Work?
The M2 Account Saver monitors your current floating loss, calculated as Balance − Equity. It activates when this loss reaches 2% of your current account balance. It is not measured from your highest equity or from the start of the trading day.
Careful position sizing can help you avoid triggering the Account Saver.
Example: With a current balance of $10,000, the M2 Account Saver triggers when floating loss reaches $200 (2%).
Event Account Status Result
First breach Drawdown reaches 2% All open trades are closed. The Funded Stage reward split is reduced to 50% , whichever phase the breach happens in.
Second breach Drawdown reaches 2% again All open trades are closed, and the account is permanently deactivated .
If you bought the 90% Profit Split add-on, the first M2 trigger still reduces your split to 50% and the add-on is lost.
