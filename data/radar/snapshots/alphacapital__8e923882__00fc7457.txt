Alpha One (1-step evaluation)
Alpha One is the single-phase path: no Phase 2. The trade-off is a trailing drawdown based on the high-water mark: 4%, 6% or 8% depending on the plan (One 6%, One 10%, One 12%; see the Alpha One rules ). The example below uses One 10% (6%).
Here is how the trailing actually works, because this is where most traders get confused:
The starting floor on a $100,000 account is $94,000 (6% below the start).
As your balance climbs, the floor trails up by the same $6,000. At $102,000 the floor moves to $96,000.
The floor keeps trailing only until your balance reaches +6% ($106,000). At that point the floor locks at your initial balance ($100,000) and never moves again.
So once you are up 6% or more, your risk floor is simply your starting balance. It does not keep tightening as you grow further.
Weekend holds on Alpha One are allowed at every stage, including the Qualified Account.
Leverage: FX 1:30, Metals 1:9, Indices 1:10, Oil 1:10. Minimum trading days: 1.

Weekend holds and overnight positions
This is one of the most misunderstood areas, so here are the exact rules.
There is no separate "overnight hold" rule. You can hold positions overnight, you simply pay or receive swap (also called rollover or overnight interest) on any position carried into the next trading day. The Qualified Account stage
Once you pass all evaluation phases, you advance to a Qualified Account with a 0% profit target. This is the qualified stage, on a simulated account, where you can request performance fees.
Performance split: depends on the plan and any add-on; performance fees are calculated from eligible simulated profit.

Max allocation: a maximum combined allocation of $400,000 across all Alpha Capital simulated accounts.
Key rules that close accounts
Maximum drawdown
If your balance falls below the drawdown floor, the account closes. Static drawdown is fixed from your initial balance and never moves. Trailing drawdown (Alpha One) follows your high-water mark and then locks at the initial balance once you are up by the drawdown percentage (6% on One 10%), as explained above. See trailing vs static drawdown .
Daily loss limit
The maximum you can lose in one trading day, measured from the start of the daily candle (00:00 GMT+2). On Alpha One and Alpha Pro 6%, it is based on the higher of that day's starting balance or equity. On Alpha Pro 8%, Alpha Pro 10%, and Alpha Swing, it is balance-based. Alpha Three (legacy plan: existing accounts only, no longer sold) is also based on the higher of that day's starting balance or equity. Either way, a breach is judged on your live equity, so open (unrealised) losses count, not just closed trades.
Max Risk Rule
The Max Risk Rule applies to Qualified Accounts bought after 21 July 2026. It limits open drawdown, meaning floating losses, on any one asset. The limit is 3% on $5,000 to $25,000 accounts, 2% on $50,000 to $200,000 accounts, and 1% on Alpha Direct at all sizes. Each asset is tracked separately, and the first one to reach its limit closes the account, however your other positions are doing.
Losing positions on the same asset are combined, and profitable ones do not offset them. All trading costs, including commissions, swaps and slippage, count towards the limit, so leave a buffer below it.
If you close a trade and open a new one on the same asset, in the same direction, within 10 minutes, the losses from both trades count together towards the limit. This carries on with each further trade, so a run of quick re-entries can form one continuous chain. The chain only ends once more than 10 minutes pass between one trade closing and the next one opening. Switching direction does not end it, because trades the other way on the same asset form a separate chain.
Minimum trading days
Alpha One requires 1 trading day. Alpha Pro, Alpha Swing, and Alpha Three (legacy plan: existing accounts only, no longer sold) require 3 per phase. A trading day counts when you open and close at least one position.
30-day inactivity
If no trade is placed for 30 consecutive days, the account is deactivated. This applies to all accounts in all stages, so keep the account active even while waiting for a setup.
Minimum trade duration (anti-HFT)
Alpha Capital expects a genuine trading style, not latency or high-frequency exploitation of the simulated feed. Your average trade duration must be above 2 minutes, and at least 50% of your profit (targeted profit during evaluations, gross profit on a Qualified Account) must come from trades held longer than 2 minutes. Occasional individual trades under 2 minutes are fine. This rule applies to all accounts. See prohibited trading strategies for the full compliance list. For what counts as hedging and how Alpha Capital treats it, see what hedging is in trading .
The 40% Best Day Rule
If you request performance fees on-demand, no single trading day can represent more than 40% of your total cumulative profit. Example: if you have $10,000 cumulative profit and your best day produced $4,500 (45%), you cannot request on-demand yet. It does not apply to bi-weekly schedules. For the maths and worked examples, see what the consistency rule is and how it works .
Common mistakes that breach accounts
Misreading Alpha One trailing. On One 10%, once you are up 6%, the floor is your starting balance, not a moving 6% below your peak. Plan your buffer around that.
Forgetting unrealised losses. Open drawdown counts toward your daily limit. Watch equity, not just closed P&L.
Stacking size into news. One bad release can erase a daily limit in seconds. Treat news as volatility, not opportunity to size up.
Not counting trading days correctly. Count days where you completed at least one round trip, not calendar days.
Leaving the account dormant. 30 days without a trade closes it.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is the maximum drawdown on Alpha Capital?
Alpha One uses a trailing drawdown from the high-water mark: 4% on One 6%, 6% on One 10% and 8% on One 12%. It locks at the starting balance once you are up by that percentage. Alpha Pro is static at 6%, 8%, or 10% depending on variant. Alpha Three (legacy plan: existing accounts only, no longer sold) is 6% static. Alpha Swing is 10% static.
What is the daily loss limit on Alpha Capital?
Alpha One 4%, Alpha Swing 5%, Alpha Pro 6% is 3%, Pro 8% is 4%, Pro 10% is 5%. Alpha One and Pro 6% use the higher of the day's starting balance or equity; Pro 8%, Pro 10%, and Swing are balance-based. Open losses always count. Alpha Three (legacy plan: existing accounts only, no longer sold) is 4% and also uses the higher of the day's starting balance or equity.
How many trading days do I need to pass Alpha Capital?
Alpha One requires 1 minimum trading day. Alpha Pro, Alpha Swing, and Alpha Three (legacy plan: existing accounts only, no longer sold) require 3 per phase. A trading day counts when at least one position is opened and closed.
Can I hold trades overnight or over the weekend on Alpha Capital?
There is no separate overnight rule, you simply pay or receive swap on positions held overnight. For weekends: Alpha One and Alpha Swing allow weekend holds at every stage, including the Qualified Account. Alpha Three (legacy plan: existing accounts only, no longer sold) also allows weekend holds at every stage. Alpha Pro allows them during the evaluation phases but not on the Qualified Account stage.
What is the difference between static and trailing drawdown?
Static drawdown is fixed from your initial balance and never moves. Trailing drawdown follows your high-water mark; on Alpha One it trails up until you are in profit by the drawdown percentage (6% on One 10%), then locks permanently at your starting balance.
What is the 40% Best Day Rule?
If you request performance fees on demand, no single trading day can represent more than 40% of your total cumulative profit. It does not apply to bi-weekly schedules.
What are the Alpha Direct rules?
Alpha Direct has no evaluation: it is a Qualified Account from purchase with a 5% trailing drawdown, a 3% daily loss limit, a 1% max risk per asset, no weekend holds and no EAs. Performance fees are on demand with a 90% performance split, after a 3% profit buffer and under a 15% Best Day Rule.

What is Slippage in Forex?
Slippage is the difference between the expected price of a trade and the price at which the trade is actually executed. It primarily occurs during periods of high volatility (like major news releases) or low liquidity (like market rollovers) when prices change faster than orders can be filled. To protect their accounts during evaluations (frequently known as prop firm challenges) and ensure consistent performance fees (or payouts), professional traders manage slippage by avoiding high-impact news and adjusting their stop-loss placements.
