How payouts work
Short answer
Once your account meets its plan's payout requirements, request a payout from the Payouts tab in your dashboard. You can be paid by crypto (up to $1,000 per request) or Rise (no maximum). A 3% processing fee applies to every payout.
The payout journey Replay
From request to payment, step by step. Payout methods, minimums and timings for each plan are in the sections below.
Before your first payout
Complete your KYC verification . It is required before any payout.
Make sure your payout details in the dashboard are correct.
To be paid through Rise (CFD and Futures), you must set up Rise before requesting a payout: create and verify your Rise account, then in your dashboard go to Settings > Payout method, add Rise as your payout method and accept the invite. You cannot request a Rise payout until Rise has been added as your payout method.

Processing and fees
Eligible payout requests are processed within 24 business hours.
Every payout has a 3% processing fee , so the amount you receive may be slightly lower than the figure on your payout certificate.
All payouts are subject to compliance, account rules and risk review.

Payout caps
Instant Starter: one payout only, capped at $250 profit. The account closes after it.
$200K, $300K and $400K Instant accounts: the first two payouts are capped at $10,000 each. After that, there is no cap.
2 Step Nano: up to 2% of your starting balance per payout cycle.
Futures payouts
All funded Futures accounts receive a 90% profit split . For example, if you make $2,000 profit, you receive $1,800.
After any approved payout, your drawdown floor locks at starting balance + $100 (for example, $50,100 on a $50,000 account).

Zero: the $8,000 daily profit limit
If a funded Zero account makes $8,000 net profit in a single trading day, it's closed and you choose between a $3,600 payout for each account that hit the limit (followed by a 30-day cooldown) or moving to Live with a Live Bonus. See Zero account rules and Zero Path to Live .
Reserve: winning days and net profit
The winning-day count and the net-profit requirement both reset after each approved payout. Net profit includes all profits and losses since your last approved payout. Example on a $50,000 account: $900 of profit and a $150 losing day gives $750 net profit, which meets the requirement. The net-profit rule only decides when you're eligible; you can still request up to 50% of your total profit, up to your cap.
