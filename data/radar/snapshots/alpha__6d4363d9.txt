What Is EOD vs Intraday Trailing Drawdown?
By the Alpha Futures Team · Updated October 23, 2026
EOD (end-of-day) trailing drawdown moves your loss limit once a day, from your closing balance. Intraday trailing drawdown moves it in real time with your highest open equity, so unrealized profit lifts the limit and giving it back can fail you mid-trade. Alpha Futures uses EOD trailing on every account, and it stops trailing at the starting balance.
Key fact Rule
Alpha Futures drawdown type EOD trailing Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) on all accounts; none use intraday trailing
When the MLL moves Once a day, from your end-of-day balance high
Open (unrealized) profit Does not lift the MLL until it is in your end-of-day balance
When a breach counts At any time: if floating equity or closed balance hits the MLL, the account is liquidated
When trailing stops When the MLL reaches the starting balance; it then stays there
Example: $50K Zero (simulated evaluation) MLL starts at $48,000. After a +$500 day it is $48,500 and stays there if you lose $500 the next day. At a $52,000 end-of-day balance it locks at $50,000

What drawdown means on a prop evaluation
Drawdown is how much your account can fall before you fail the attempt or breach Qualified Account rules. On futures prop programmes the main label is often MLL (Maximum Loss Limit).
Two different update clocks change how it feels to trade:
EOD trailing (session close)
Intraday trailing (live during the session)
EOD trailing drawdown (end-of-day)
How it works:
Your high watermark updates from end-of-session balance
Open profit during the day does not permanently lift MLL until the session marks
Give-back late in the day does not move the MLL level until the close, but the MLL still applies live: if you break it at any point, on floating equity or closed balance, the account is liquidated
Example (simplified): Start $25,000 simulated with $1,000 EOD trailing MLL. Floor is $24,000. You close the day +$800. Next session the floor may trail up based on the EOD rules.
Trader fit: Scalpers and day traders who want intraday wiggle room on MLL (still subject to other rules).
Intraday trailing drawdown
How it works:
MLL can trail up while you are in a trade as balance hits new highs
A sharp reversal can breach MLL mid-session even if you were green minutes earlier
Feels tighter for active scalping and fast mean-reversion
Trader fit: Often suits traders who lock profits quickly or trade smaller size. Less forgiving on open P&L give-back.
We do not claim which third-party firms use which model. Read each operator’s official MLL article.

Daily Loss Guard (DLG) is a soft breach rule, separate from EOD MLL: if you hit it, you do not lose the account, but it is locked until the next trading day (6PM ET).
Zero: DLG is 2% of the starting balance ($500 / $1,000 / $2,000 on 25K / 50K / 100K), including on evaluations. Direct: $500 / $1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000 (25K / 50K / 100K / 150K). Standard: no DLG on evaluations; $1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000 (50K / 100K / 150K) otherwise.
Advanced: No DLG on evaluation or Qualified Accounts.

FAQs
Does Alpha Futures use intraday trailing MLL?
No. MLL is end-of-day trailing on all plans.
Is EOD trailing “easier” than intraday?
Often on MLL alone, yes, because open profit does not instantly ratchet the floor. Zero DLG can still stop your day early.
What is static drawdown?
Some firms use a fixed floor that never trails up. Alpha Futures uses EOD trailing, not static MLL, on current plans.
