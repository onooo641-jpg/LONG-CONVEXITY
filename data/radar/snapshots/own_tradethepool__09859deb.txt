Basics and Prerequisites
Purpose and Goals
Trade The Pool is centered around enabling and growing consistently profitable stock traders.
The Professional programs, including the passing criteria for the evaluation phase and the general trading requirements, are designed to allow the maximum flexibility for the trader while fitting with TTP’s risk management requirements. While some rules may seem arbitrary from the standpoint of the individual trader, they are set up to protect the funding pool for all traders against even “edge cases” or “perfect storms”.
TTP welcomes feedback and continually evaluates efficacy of the rules along with overall trader performance; however, at the end of the day it is the User’s responsibility to adapt strategy and trading style to the specific rules as currently defined. This is what allows TTP to offer these opportunities to traders.
Document Structure and Interpretation
This document is intended to allow the User to understand the structure of the overall framework and specific Professional programs, along with the spirit and letter of individual rules.
While every effort has been made to make the purpose and intent of the rules clear, the rules themselves are what matters.
This document contains 2 kinds of information:
normative content is prescriptive, providing rules and requirements which must be followed
non-normative content (also called “informative content”) provides background, examples, notes, and other information
Because examples are specific rather than general, they may not illustrate all aspects or be the only way to approach a given situation. Therefore all examples are non-normative, and the wording of the rule itself always takes precedence.

Once you are qualified for a Professional account, every time you satisfy the scaling criteria we will increase your Buying Power and Daily Pause.

Volatility Rules
Actively trading is not permitted during an applicable trading halt.
An applicable trading halt consists of any of the following:
A trading pause, for any reason, on the specific symbol
A market-wide circuit breaker or any other exchange- or market-wide trading outage
Any condition disrupting a fair and orderly market, on a given symbol or the market as a whole, which may be declared in advance or at the time by TTP in its sole judgment at its sole discretion
Any condition causing the trading system to display a symbol as untradeable
For the purposes of this section, each symbol affected by such a halt is designated a halted symbol and a position (actual or potential) on any such symbol is designated a halted position.
For the purposes of this section, actively trading consists of any of the following:
The execution of any order on a halted symbol
(Note: Execution of such orders during a technically implemented halt is generally impossible but, in the event the system inadvertently purports to record such execution, TTP reserves the right to reverse or delete the trade in question)
Placing an order to open or add to a halted position
It is the User’s responsibility to be aware of potential halts, and to manage open positions and orders appropriately.
A symbol may be subject to an automatic halt, without notice, after the asset moved 10% within 5 regular trading minutes.
Users should familiarize themselves with regulatory and exchange halt rules (e.g., Nasdaq LULD).
Minimum Volume Rule (The 5% Rule) is as follows:
The volume of any opening trades (opening or adding to a position) must not exceed 5% of the trading volume in the previous one-minute candle for that instrument.
The total volume of any new position in an instrument (including partial entries and multiple orders) must not exceed 5% of the trading volume in the previous one-minute candle for that instrument, as measured by the data provided in our system.
Opening multiple positions within the same one-minute period, or layering entries in an instrument that collectively exceed the 5% limit, is prohibited.
For enforcement, all trades executed in the same instrument within the same minute are aggregated and treated as a single position.
Enforcement:
Any trades in an instrument that violate this rule, whether through a single oversized order or multiple smaller orders intended to bypass the limit, may be invalidated and could result in additional restrictions or account review.
Consistency/Validity Rules
The maximum position profit ratio is 30% unless otherwise specified; that is, the User’s best position cannot be responsible for more than 30% (or the relevant ratio) of the total valid profit earned. This applies to all evaluations, and to payouts and scaling from those Professional accounts with this rule.
For evaluation accounts, the User should close each position via pending limit orders or other appropriate mechanisms prior to this amount, relative to the profit target (original or adjusted; see below).
Note: This is ideal, because it demonstrates the discipline the Company is looking for and makes the evaluation easier for the User, but not required.
The User must earn sufficient overall simulated profit so that this ratio is not exceeded by any retained position relative to overall profit, regardless of whether any position profit exceeds the ratio relative to the profit target.
The lowest profit amount which allows this condition to be satisfied for all valid trades is the adjusted profit target.
Some account types (see Detailed Rules and Additional Parameters) impose a requirement of a minimum number of profitable days in a payout cycle.
In order for profit from any position to be counted, the following distance and duration requirements must be met:
Each position must yield at least 10 price ticks of profit (that is, 10.0 cents difference between average entry and average exit price).
Example:
Buy 100 at $50.10, sell 100 at $50.20 (at least).
Buy 100 at $50.10, buy 100 at $50.15, sell 200 at $50.23 (at least).
There must be at least a 60.0-second range (except otherwise specified, including 30.0 seconds for MAX/FLEX) between each opening/adding execution and the next subsequent closing/reducing execution over the lifetime of that position.
Note: All rules corresponding to “positions” apply to any profit from the entire lifetime of any position (i.e., from the opening trade until completely “flat” again).

Payouts
Company pays the Professional User a Reward calculated as an agreed portion of the hypothetical net profits recorded in the Professional Account, upon request, a minimum of 14 days since the last payout (or, for the first payout on a given account, since beginning Professional trading or the creation of a newly scaled account). Payouts will be paid only to a Professional User who has a balance of $300.00 or more in the account (after subtracting any starting virtual capital) after closing all positions and orders, and the payout will be calculated only according to the profitable performance of the Users according to the Trading Rules.
Any transaction or withdrawal of profits may be subject to a processing fee.
In order to ensure you build up an adequate buffer and stay trading for a longer period, once the account has reached 3 x DLs in equity (i.e., “projected balance” including unrealized profits) the max drawdown for the account will move to the initial balance, going below which will result in the account being terminated.
It is permitted to bypass this by withdrawing prior to reaching this threshold, but be aware that building a buffer is the preferred model and that the move is performed instantly based on intraday projected balance.
For example (for reference only; the specific terms above control):
Assume for this example an account with $100,000 initial balance, $2000 DL and $4000 max DD (minimum equity $96,000).
When this account reaches $106,000 in equity the minimum equity will now move to $100,000 without a buffer.
If after this point the trader decides to withdraw the full $6000, or the projected balance on the account moves below $100,000, the account will be terminated.
To continue to trade the trader needs to ensure that after any withdrawal there is an amount greater than $100,000 remaining, which will provide the sole drawdown buffer.
For legacy accounts with a starting balance of $0, the process is the same (minimum equity moves to $0, so going negative terminates the account).
Scaling
Scaling of Professional accounts depends on the account type.
Previously split profit is not subject to additional splitting upon payout/scaling.
Deductions against previously split profits are permanent; profitable trades which match or exceed these losses do not replenish the previously split profit but generate new profit. Accordingly, a realized loss within a given scaling tier which was not preceded by greater realized profits in that scaling tier will require additional profit to restore the same withdrawable amount. These deductions include anything which reduces the balance, when insufficient new profit remains in the account to cover them:
Commissions
Realized trading losses
Payouts
Each time the account balance reaches a new lowest point below the starting balance, the corresponding portion of previously split profit will be reduced.
Upon each Scaling Event, the applicable Profit Split (70% Customer / 30% TTP) is applied to remaining eligible Profit. The User’s portion is posted to the next-level Scaled Account as Credits.
Credits are applied on a first-loss basis. To the extent equity falls below the Initial Balance, the loss is charged against Credits until exhausted.
Subsequent gains or recoveries do not restore previously reduced or exhausted Credits. Profits generated after a Scaling Event are treated as new profit and are subject to the then applicable Profit Split and reward/scaling rules.
MAX/FLEX
Only day-trading accounts available; swing to come.
The tier balance is the nominal portion of the notional capital providing the buying power to the account, excluding both trading profits and any drawdown once available profits have been exhausted.
For the first tier, the tier balance is the same as the notional capital providing the buying power to the account. The scaled account will have a starting trading balance of the previous tier balance plus 5% (which becomes the new tier balance), plus the User’s portion of the profit earned in the previous tier. The max drawdown in the scaled account is set again to the overall loss limit for the account type, meaning the minimum equity is the new tier balance minus the overall loss limit. For example:
A newly Professional $200k FLEX account earns $20k valid profit and fulfills all scaling criteria. The account is closed and a new account is opened, with a starting trading balance of $224k: the tier balance of $210k (the previous tier balance + 5%) plus $14k of rolled-over profit (70% of $20k earned in previous tier). The max drawdown is $22k, meaning the minimum equity is $202,000.

If the User requests payout of $5k (of account balance, potentially pre-split), a new account is again created. The starting trading balance is $227,500: the same tier balance plus 17,500(14k old profit + 70% of the remaining $5000 of new profit). The trader receives $3500 (70% of $5000 requested). The max drawdown is $25,500, leaving minimum equity the same at $202,000.
Note: As described elsewhere, whenever 3 × DL of new profit is earned (whether realized or unrealized) in any of these accounts, the max drawdown is reset to the amount of rolled-over profit (whether $0 or some other amount). This makes the minimum equity the same as the tier balance, in this example $200,000 for the initial Professional account or $210,000 for the first scaled account, meaning that the User may lose all earned profit but no more (until the next splitting event results in a new account).

Stop Loss and Quantity
Stop and Quantity:
Daily loss X 30% = the maximum $ risk you can take on each trade (each execution)
Entry Price – Stop Loss Price = Stop size
Max $ Risk / Stop size = Number of shares to buy/sell
Example: Stock price is $100, Entry price $101, Stop loss at $99.
Stop size = $2
Daily loss = $700
Max money risk 30% of Daily Loss = $210
Quantity = $210 / $2 = 105 shares to buy/sell

Daily Loss Limit
The account Daily Loss is the current equity (projected balance) at each moment minus the balance (realized) at the start of the day. Each account has a Daily Loss Limit determined by the account type and any scaling. The system may provide the User an opportunity to reset the Daily Pause, which will allow trading to resume with an additional Daily Loss Limit’s worth of losses allowed in the current trading day.

Is the account value by equity, which determines the maximum loss allowed in the account.
Once the Account Stop out value is breached below, termination for the account will be announced, all unrealized positions, and orders will be closed by the authorized personnel.

Minimum Volume Per Asset
Regular Trading Volume
During regular trading hours, a User may open or add to a position only if the number of shares that have been traded on that day is more than 200,000 shares.
Example:
ABC ticker has a volume of 150,000 at 09:50 ET – must not open/add
ABC ticker has a volume of 203,000 at 09:57 ET – may open/add
Extended Trading Volume
During extended trading (pre-/after-market), a User may open or add to a position only if the number of shares that have been traded on that day is more than 20,000 shares.
Example:
ABC ticker has a volume of 15,000 at 08:50 ET – must not open/add
ABC ticker has a volume of 23,000 at 08:53 ET – may open/add
Special Case – Threshold Reached During the Entry Minute
If an order is opened in an instrument during a minute when its daily trading volume had not yet met the required threshold but the threshold was reached during that same minute, the following calculation is applied:
Take the total trading volume of that instrument in the one-minute candle.
Divide the total candle volume by 60 seconds (per-second average).
Multiply the per-second average by the number of seconds elapsed in the candle at the time of entry.
Add this to the instrument’s daily volume at the start of the candle.
If the calculated daily volume for that instrument at the entry time meets or exceeds the required minimum (400k in regular trading hours, 40k in extended hours), the trade is valid. Otherwise, it is invalid.
Example:
Valid trade:
Daily volume before candle = 398,000.
Entry at 20 seconds into the candle.
Candle adds 12,000 shares → 200 shares/second.
200 × 20 = 4,000 → 398,000 + 4,000 = 402,000.
Trade valid (threshold met).
Invalid Trade:
Same candle, entry at 10 seconds.
200 × 10 = 2,000 → 398,000 + 2,000 = 400,000 (still below threshold).
Trade invalid.

Trading Conditions
Trading performance may also be affected by various conditions, as such:
Variable Spread
Spreads vary according to the market liquidity, at rigid times such as during high-impact economic events, the spread difference of ask and bid prices can expand multiple times.
Simulated spreads are derived from market data provided by third-party data providers reflecting Nasdaq pricing.
Stop Pending Orders
Ordering at a specifically requested price is not a functionality of stop orders – once triggered, the simulated order will be filled at the next available price according to the market data recorded in the Simulated Environment.
Trading Gaps
At times of extreme liquidity in the market, the differences from one tick quote for the next may significantly increase. This is also known as ‘A Gap’. Any orders that are placed at prices with no tick quote will be filled at the next available quote. This may result in execution at a less preferred price.
Economic Event Releases
At times of releasing any Economic Data, the price tends to form gaps, and spreads may increase in multiples, and execution is not guaranteed by price. It is advised to pay attention to the event release schedule, to reduce position exposure and shift further limit and stop orders.
