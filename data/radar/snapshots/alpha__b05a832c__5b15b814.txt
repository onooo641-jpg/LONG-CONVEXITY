Alpha Futures: plans, rules, and platforms
Alpha Futures is a UK-based futures prop firm and part of the Alpha Group alongside Alpha Capital Group. Since launching in 2024, it has published evaluation and Qualified rules across Zero, Standard, Advanced, and Direct.
Plans and account structure
Alpha Futures currently offers three one-step evaluation plans—Zero, Standard, and Advanced—and a separate Direct Qualified path that skips evaluation. Premium was discontinued in July 2026. All accounts use simulated funds.
Zero
No evaluation consistency rule; 40% consistency on the Qualified Account between performance fee requests. Daily Loss Guard applies. See the current Zero plan .
Standard
50% consistency during evaluation and 40% on the Qualified Account between performance fee requests. See the current Standard plan .
Advanced
40% consistency during evaluation and no consistency rule on the Qualified Account. No preset Daily Loss Guard; the performance fee cap is up to $15,000 per request. See the current Advanced plan .
Direct
Direct skips evaluation and starts on a Qualified Account. A 20% consistency rule applies for performance fee eligibility. See the current Direct plan .

Rules that matter
Alpha Futures allows news trading on all evaluation accounts. On Qualified Accounts, Advanced Qualified Accounts have no news restrictions; Zero Qualified Accounts restrict new orders within 2 minutes before or after high-impact news (ForexFactory red-folder events). You may hold through news on Zero Qualified Accounts — the restriction applies to executing new orders in that window.
Hedging and reverse trading are prohibited on evaluation and Qualified stages. Overnight and session-hold rules vary by firm.
The 50% consistency applies on Standard evaluations and 40% on Advanced evaluations; it does not apply on Advanced Qualified Accounts. Zero evaluations have no consistency rule; Zero Qualified Accounts use a 40% consistency rule between performance fee requests.
All current plans carry a 90% performance split from day one, no tiered ladder. Performance fees are requestable up to 4 times per month after every 5 winning trading days of $200 or more.
Alpha Futures currently supports AlphaTrader, TradingView (Plus500), WealthCharts, DeepChart, and Quantower. Active communities run on Facebook, X, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok, and Discord.

What to look for in a futures prop firm
Before you choose a firm from the table, know the six criteria that separate programmes worth your evaluation fee from programmes worth skipping.
Rules transparency
Can you read the profit target, drawdown model, consistency rule, and prohibited practices in plain language before you pay? At Alpha Futures, Standard evaluations have a 50% consistency rule, Advanced evaluations 40%, Zero evaluations none, and Zero Qualified Accounts 40%. Firms that bury rules in PDFs or change them without notice are a red flag.
Drawdown model
End-of-day trailing drawdown, intraday trailing drawdown, and static drawdown behave very differently in practice. Alpha Futures uses end-of-day trailing Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) on all plans—not intraday trailing. On Zero, MLL is 4% ($1K / $2K / $3K by size). On Advanced, 3.5% ($1,750 / $3,500 / $5,250). Check current Standard and Direct loss limits on their plan cards.
Performance fee reliability
A 90% performance split means little if the firm delays or denies requests. Check Trustpilot scores, independent review sites, and trader communities alongside the published performance fee policy.
Platform quality
Your execution environment affects fills, DOM access, and charting workflow. Alpha Futures currently offers AlphaTrader, TradingView (Plus500), WealthCharts, DeepChart, and Quantower. More platform choice means less friction when you qualify and scale.
Trustpilot rating and review volume
A high score on many reviews carries more weight than the same score on a handful of reviews. Volume matters because it reduces the impact of fake or incentivised reviews—use it as one filter, not the only one.
Account sizes and scaling
Entry-level evaluations often start at $25K or $50K simulated capital. Alpha Futures offers account sizes from $25K to $150K across three active evaluation plans: Zero, Standard, and Advanced. Direct provides a separate Qualified entry without an evaluation; Premium was discontinued in July 2026.
Frequently asked questions
Which futures prop firm is best for beginners?
One with a low-cost evaluation, micro contracts and rules you can follow. At Alpha Futures, Zero 25K costs $89 a month with no activation fee and no consistency rule on the evaluation. See our futures prop firms for beginners guide.
What is the cheapest futures prop firm?
The one with the lowest total cost to reach a funded stage for you, counting evaluation fees, resets and activation. Compare the 50K costs in the table above; at Alpha Futures, Zero 25K costs $89 a month with no activation fee. See our cheapest futures prop firm guide.
Are futures prop firm accounts simulated?
At Alpha Futures, yes: evaluations and Qualified Accounts are simulated and do not give access to live markets. Check each firm’s terms for how its funded stage works.
End-of-day vs intraday drawdown: what’s the difference?
End-of-day trailing drawdown moves your loss limit once a day from your closing balance; intraday trailing drawdown follows your highest open equity during the session, so profit you give back mid-trade can tighten the limit. Alpha Futures uses end-of-day trailing on every account. See EOD vs intraday trailing drawdown .
Which futures prop firms have no consistency rule?
At Alpha Futures, Zero evaluations and Advanced Qualified Accounts have no consistency rule. In the table, the Apex and Tradeify evaluations, Lucid’s funded stage and Topstep’s Standard funded path have none. See futures prop firms with no consistency rule .
How often can you request a payout?
At Alpha Futures, up to 4 times a month, after 5 winning days of $200 or more, for up to 50% of profit per request, capped by plan. The other firms’ payout conditions are in the table above.
