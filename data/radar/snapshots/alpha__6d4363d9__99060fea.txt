Futures Prop Firms With No Consistency Rule (2026)
By the Alpha Futures Team · Updated October 23, 2026
At Alpha Futures, two account stages have no consistency rule: Zero evaluations and Advanced Qualified Accounts. Every other stage has one: 50% on Standard evaluations, 40% on Advanced evaluations and on Zero and Standard Qualified Accounts, and 20% on Direct. The Maximum Loss Limit still applies everywhere, so no consistency rule does not mean no rules.
Updated 23 October 2026. Alpha Futures currently offers Zero, Standard, Advanced, and Direct. Premium was discontinued in July 2026.
Part of our futures prop rules series. Start with what the consistency rule is , then read the consistency calculator · performance fee rules · how to pass a futures prop evaluation · Maximum Loss Limit · how it works .
What is a consistency rule?
A consistency rule limits how much of your total net profit may come from your best trading day. The ratio can affect when you pass an evaluation or become eligible for a performance fee request. It is separate from the profit target, Maximum Loss Limit , and Daily Loss Guard.
Alpha Futures consistency rules by plan
Current plan Evaluation Qualified Account
Zero No consistency rule 40% between performance fee requests
Standard 50% 40% between performance fee requests
Advanced 40% No consistency rule
Direct No evaluation 20% for performance fee eligibility
On the Standard Plan, the optional No Consistency in Qualified add-on removes the 40% consistency rule from your simulated Standard Qualified Account; the Standard Evaluation keeps its 50% rule. See No Consistency in Qualified: the Standard Plan add-on .
Premium was a past plan and is not available for new purchases.
What "no consistency rule" can mean
Plans do not all mean the same thing by "no consistency rule".
No rule during the evaluation. Zero is this case. You can reach the profit target in one day. The Qualified Account still has a 40% rule later.
No rule on the Qualified Account. Advanced is this case. The evaluation has a 40% rule. After you qualify, that rule drops off.
No rule at either stage. Alpha Futures does not offer that. Direct skips the evaluation and then uses 20% before a performance fee request.
What still applies on Zero
No consistency rule on the Zero evaluation does not remove the other rules. These are the published Zero 50K figures.
Rule Zero 50K
Profit target on the evaluation $3,000
Maximum Loss Limit $2,000
Daily Loss Guard $1,000
Consistency on the evaluation None
Consistency on the Qualified Account 40% since the last performance fee request
Passing a Zero 50K evaluation in one day
You make $3,000 in one session. That meets the profit target. Zero has no consistency rule on the evaluation, so that one day can be the whole target.
Two rules still apply on that day. The account fails if it hits the Maximum Loss Limit, which starts $2,000 under the $50,000 balance. The Daily Loss Guard stops the session at a $1,000 loss and locks new trades for the day. It does not, by itself, close the account.
After you are on the Qualified Account, a new window starts. The 40% rule applies to profit since the last performance fee request. A share of 40% or more is not eligible yet. If the best day in that window is $1,200, the total has to be above $3,000 before a request can pass the rule. $1,200 is 40% of $3,000, so that total is still short. The account is not breached for the ratio. You keep trading.

Which plan fits your goal?
Pass an evaluation without a best-day ratio
Consider Zero. You still need to hit its profit target and stay inside its Maximum Loss Limit and Daily Loss Guard. The absence of an evaluation consistency rule does not remove other plan rules.
Trade without a qualified-stage consistency ratio
Consider Advanced. Its evaluation has a 40% consistency rule, but its Qualified Account has none. This differs from Standard and Zero, which use 40% after qualification.
Start Qualified without an evaluation
Direct skips evaluation but applies 20% consistency for performance fee eligibility. Compare its price, profit target, limits, and performance fee conditions on the Direct plan .
Common mistakes
Assuming no consistency rule means no loss limit or no trading restrictions.
Reading the evaluation rule and overlooking the Qualified Account rule.
Using an old Premium comparison as if Premium were still sold.
Treating a completed profit target as a confirmed pass before all applicable conditions are met.
FAQs
Does Alpha Futures have an evaluation with no consistency rule?
Yes. Zero evaluations have no consistency rule. Direct skips the evaluation entirely.
Does Advanced have no consistency rule?
Advanced Qualified Accounts have no consistency rule. The Advanced evaluation has a 40% consistency rule.
What is the 50% consistency rule at Alpha Futures?
It applies to Standard evaluations. The largest profitable day must meet the ratio set in the current policy before the evaluation can pass.
What happened to Premium?
Premium was discontinued in July 2026. Current choices are Zero, Standard, Advanced, and Direct.
All accounts use simulated funds. Eligible performance fees depend on the published rules; outcomes are not guaranteed.
