Welcome to Across
Welcome to Across
Move money across all major chains.
Copy Markdown Open with AI
Across moves money between chains. You tell it what you want to move and where
it should land; it prices that transfer, tells you what the user has to
authorize, and reports what happened to the funds until the outcome is settled.
Every quote follows a similar schema, which defines the tokens that are moving
and which origin and destination chains are being used. Assets, chains and
authorization methods are parameters in the latest Across API that the developer
can configure.

The integration flow
Up to four calls, in this order. Each one hands you what the next one needs.
Call 3 is needed only when the quote's nextActions ends with submit_execution .
Call What you get back
1 GET /v1/capabilities What can move between two chains right now
2 POST /v1/quotes The price of one exact transfer, and the ordered actions needed to execute it
3 POST /v1/quotes/{orderId}/executions Only when nextActions ends with submit_execution : confirmation that the authorization was accepted
4 GET /v1/transfers/{orderId} Status of the funds
Utilizing returned data at each integration step
1. Capabilities is your starting point. This endpoint defines the available
paths for a given input↔output token combination and the related
origin↔destination chain combination. Read decimals per token — the same asset
can differ per chain, so never hardcode it. Anchor all four sides
( originChainId , originToken , destinationChainId , destinationToken ) and
the response also carries a capabilities object with the executionOptions
and amountTypes that route actually serves, plus the limits it will price.
2. The quote defines the order that will be executed. nextActions is an
ordered list of what has to happen before the transfer can proceed; walk it in
order and collect what each step produces. Mind the quote expiry in expiresAt
and do not submit stale quotes, always request a fresh one instead.
3. Execution submits what the actions produced, when the quote asks for it.
If nextActions ends with submit_execution , POST the results (usually a
signature) to the executions endpoint. Its response confirms the transfer is
being processed. If it doesn't, there is nothing to submit: a quote funded by
an approval and a prefunded-store transaction starts as soon as that
transaction lands on chain. Either way, track the transfer by the quote's
orderId .
4. Transfer tracks the latest status of your transaction. Poll until
status.isTerminal is true: the outcome is known and you can stop. Show status.description to users as-is; it is
written for that. Branch your own logic on status.state and status.reason , never on the
description text.
The shape of this API is subject to feedback and may change.
Defining your transfer
The calls explained above, in order, will move real money
along any route the system can price, on whatever terms you configure. What you send is what gets executed, so the
configuration you supply at each step is the whole of the decision.
Here is what is yours to decide, in the order you will meet it.
1. Capabilities: establish what is possible. Choose your input and output
tokens and the origin and destination chains. Read back the available
information for that pair: the executionOptions it supports, the amountTypes
it serves, and the limits it will price.
2. Quote: commit to an amount. Define exactly how much money you want to
move, along with the details you took from step 1. amountType decides which
side carries the amount. You don't name a funding method: declare what your
client can do in capabilities.operations , optionally narrowed by
authorizationPolicy , and the server picks an eligible method.
3. Execute: authorize it correctly. Follow nextActions in order, and
submit the results only if they end with submit_execution . Make sure a
signature is formed over exactly the data the quote handed you; that payload
is what the server verifies, so it must be signed verbatim.
4. Track: follow it until it is terminal. Poll the transfer until
status.isTerminal is true rather than assuming a successful submission means delivered
funds.
