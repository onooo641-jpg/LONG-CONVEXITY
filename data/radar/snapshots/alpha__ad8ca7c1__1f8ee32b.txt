What is the Consistency Rule? (2026)
By the Alpha Futures Team · Updated October 23, 2026
A consistency rule limits how much of your net profit can come from your single best trading day. At Alpha Futures it depends on plan and stage: 50% on Standard evaluations, 40% on Advanced evaluations and on Zero and Standard Qualified Accounts, 20% on Direct, and none on Zero evaluations or Advanced Qualified Accounts.
Key fact At Alpha Futures
What it measures Your best day's profit as a share of your net profit
Evaluations Standard 50%, Advanced 40%, Zero none. You pass only when you reach the profit target and meet the rule.
Qualified Accounts Zero and Standard 40%, Direct 20%, Advanced none. Measured on profit since your last request.
Going over it on a Qualified Account The account is not breached. You keep trading until the rule is met.
Futures trading involves substantial risk. Alpha Futures evaluations and Qualified Accounts are simulated; past simulated results do not guarantee future results.
Updated October 23, 2026: Current plans are Zero, Standard, Advanced, and Direct. Premium was discontinued in July 2026.
Current Alpha Futures consistency rules
Plan Evaluation Qualified Account
Zero None 40% between performance fee requests
Standard 50% 40% between performance fee requests
Advanced 40% None
Direct No evaluation 20% for performance fee eligibility
On the Standard Plan, the optional No Consistency in Qualified add-on removes the 40% consistency rule from your simulated Standard Qualified Account; the Standard Evaluation keeps its 50% rule. See No Consistency in Qualified: the Standard Plan add-on .
What is the 50% consistency rule?
The 50% consistency rule means no single trading day can be more than 50% of your net profit. At Alpha Futures it applies to Standard evaluations. On a simulated $50K Standard evaluation with a $3,000 profit target, keep your best day at $1,500 or less so you can pass as soon as you reach the target. If your best day is $2,000, you need at least $4,000 of net profit before you pass, even though the target is $3,000.
Standard evaluation (simulated) Profit target Best day to pass at the target
$50K $3,000 $1,500 or less
$100K $6,000 $3,000 or less
$150K $9,000 $4,500 or less
How the ratio works
Suppose your best day is $2,500 and your net profit is $4,000. That day accounts for 62.5% of net profit. It would exceed a 50% Standard evaluation limit and a 40% Advanced evaluation limit. More eligible net profit spread across other trading days would be needed before the applicable ratio is satisfied. The profit target, loss limits, and other plan rules still apply.
What happens if you break the consistency rule?
On a Qualified Account, breaking the consistency rule does not breach the account. You just can't request a performance fee until your best day is back under the limit, which happens as you add net profit on other days. The ratio counts profit since your last request and resets after each request. In an evaluation, you don't pass until the rule is met. To check your numbers, use the consistency rule calculator .
Evaluation and Qualified rules are different
Zero has no evaluation consistency rule, but its Qualified Account uses 40% between performance fee requests. Advanced uses 40% during evaluation and none after qualification. Standard uses 50% during evaluation and 40% after qualification. Direct skips evaluation but uses 20% for performance fee eligibility.
Consistency versus other rules
Profit target: the net profit you must reach to pass an evaluation.
Consistency: the allowed concentration of profit in your best day.
Maximum Loss Limit: the account loss threshold.
Daily Loss Guard: a separate daily control on plans where it applies.
Frequently asked questions
Does Zero have an evaluation consistency rule?
No. Zero Qualified Accounts do have a 40% rule between performance fee requests.
What is the Advanced evaluation consistency rule?
Advanced evaluations use 40%; Advanced Qualified Accounts have no consistency rule.
What is the Standard evaluation consistency rule?
Standard evaluations use 50%; Standard Qualified Accounts use 40% between performance fee requests.
Does Direct have an evaluation consistency rule?
Direct has no evaluation. Its Qualified Account has a 20% consistency rule for performance fee eligibility.
